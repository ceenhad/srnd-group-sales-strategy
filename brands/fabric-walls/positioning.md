# Fabric Walls — brand strategy

*Phase-1 clear strategy for Fabric Walls: the settled positioning that content development and
deployment build against. This document is **internal** — nothing here is website copy as it stands.
Each section has a **positioning core**, written so it could become public copy, and the **internal
reasoning** under it. Grounded in the `fabricwalls.uk` website (the reference standard), not the older
brand-guidelines documents.*

---

## 1. What Fabric Walls is

**Positioning core**

> Fabric Walls is a faster, more reliable way to build fabric interiors. The frames are engineered and
> assembled in the factory, made to the exact size of the room; the fabric arrives machine-cut, with a
> silicone edge that pushes into place by hand. No cutting on site, no tucking tools, no specialist
> upholsterers — if a team can fit furniture or AV, they can install Fabric Walls, to the same standard
> every time. It's the finished surface a room is built into: screens, speakers, lighting, wiring and
> acoustic treatment all integrate with it — some set into it, some concealed behind.

**The reasoning under it**

- **What it really is: a craft turned into a product.** Building fabric interiors used to mean skilled
  work sweated over on site. Fabric Walls engineers that into a factory-prepared, push-fit system that
  goes up predictably. The value is removing the risk, the time and the graft from a premium finish —
  and it's bought as a finished product, not a box of bits.
- **Fabric Walls has calculators and a trade store — and should.** It's a specify-configure-and-supply
  product, so self-serve ordering is right for it. This is the deliberate *opposite* of C-ATS, where
  design is paid consulting and calculators would commoditise it. Same group, different mechanic — the
  no-calculator rule does **not** carry across.
- **The Method is the brand's structure:** Fabric Panels → Fabric Wall Elements → Interior Kits →
  Levels 1–4, with the kits sold as city-named schemes. A clear building-block story, good for content.
- **Acoustic behaviour is engineered to the spec and tested in-house** — transparent where sound must
  pass through, absorptive where the room needs damping. Never a blanket "transparent" claim (see §4).
- **Not fenced — to cinema or to walls.** "Walls" is just the name; the system goes on ceilings and
  other surfaces too. Markets run from home cinema, media rooms and DCI screening to luxury living and
  commercial fit-out. Shallow (≈35 mm), with brackets out to ~200 mm for speakers or treatment behind.
- **Provenance:** the current site was built ad-hoc, no plan — a placeholder to re-base against this
  strategy, not preserved as intent.

## 2. Who it's for, and what their problem really is

**Positioning core**

> A fabric wall is the first thing anyone sees when they walk into the room, and the one surface with
> nowhere to hide a mistake. That's what makes it an uncomfortable job. Not that it's hard to grasp —
> it's that the work is slow, physical and unforgiving, and the cheapest way to do it, tracking fabric
> on site, is the most nerve-wracking of all. Fabric Walls changes what the job is. Instead of a craft
> to be sweated over on site, it becomes a product: built to the exact room in the factory, and pushed
> into place by hand. The finish is settled before anyone reaches site — which is why AV integrators
> and fit-out contractors alike would rather fit one than build one.

**The reasoning under it**

- **It's a fear-and-labour problem, not a skill problem.** Installers can do fabric work; the trouble
  is it's high-stakes (visible, easy to ruin) and physically hard — worse with cheap on-site track.
  Never frame it as the dealer lacking skill. Fabric Walls sells calm and saved effort, not rescue.
- **The alternative it beats is on-site fabric track.** Cheaper on materials, but more risk and more
  labour. Fabric Walls' price is justified by removing both — that's the trade-off it wins.
- **It's a product sale, not a collection of bits.** This is why fit-out contractors take to it: it
  drops into a fit-out as a predictable purchased product, not a craft exercise.
- **The visible-finish stakes hold** (the opposite of C-ATS's invisible acoustics) — a mistake is
  instantly obvious, which is what makes the reassurance worth paying for.
- **The buyer widens beyond AV:** integrator plus fit-out contractor, both valuing predictability over
  craft.

## 3. The promise

**Positioning core**

> Fabric Walls settles the result before the work begins. The room is specified, and the system comes
> back made precisely to it — every frame sized, every panel of fabric cut and labelled in the factory.
> What used to be days of careful work on site becomes a quick, orderly assembly, because the difficult
> part is already finished. The integrator hands over a flawless finish with the rest of the room
> integrated cleanly into it, on a date they could promise a client — and delivers it again, exactly,
> on the next job.

**The reasoning under it**

- **Time is a headline benefit, not a footnote.** The install is a fraction of what it would otherwise
  take. That means lower labour cost, less site disruption, and — just as important — a schedule the
  integrator can actually commit to and keep.
- **The promise is certainty.** Where C-ATS promises a *verifiable* result, Fabric Walls promises a
  *predetermined* one — finish and timeline fixed in the factory, not discovered on site.
- **Don't over-lead on effort or skill.** Reduced graft and a lower skill barrier are real, but
  secondary, and leaning on them risks implying the dealer can't cope. Lead with certainty and time.
- **Support is a choice, never a rescue.** Self-serve calculators and trade store, Design & Technical
  Planning, or Approved Partner Installation — the dealer picks their level of involvement.
- **Honesty line:** the finish and acoustic-behaviour claims rest on factory precision, the push-fit
  silicone edge, and in-house lab testing. Don't claim beyond that.

## 4. Why it's credible

**Positioning core**

> The proof of Fabric Walls is that you can see it. The finish speaks for itself — and it holds to the
> same standard across completely different rooms, because it's a manufactured product, cut and
> assembled to the millimetre in the factory, not a result that depends on who happened to install it.
> And whatever the room needs the wall to do acoustically — let the sound of hidden speakers through,
> or absorb sound to settle the space — that behaviour is measured in our own lab, not assumed. Anyone
> who wants to judge it before they specify it can stand in a finished room and do exactly that.

**The reasoning under it**

- **Credibility works the opposite way to C-ATS.** C-ATS must prove an *invisible* result with
  measurement; Fabric Walls' quality is *visible*, so the finish is its own proof. Lead with that.
- **Manufacturing is the substance, and consistency means approach and result — never design.**
  UK-made, CNC-cut aluminium frames to exact size, factory-assembled and labelled. What's consistent is
  the quality and the predictability of the outcome. The look is deliberately flexible — that's what the
  Method and the city-named schemes are for. The standard never varies; the design always can.
- **Acoustics is application-specific — handle with care.** Cinema wants transparency (speakers behind
  the fabric); much commercial work wants the reverse — the fabric plus Acoustic Core as the absorber
  that damps the room. Never present transparency as a universal property; it misleads and it re-fences
  us to cinema. The wall is tested for whichever behaviour the spec calls for, and that flexibility is
  itself a selling point beyond cinema.
- **The Experience Centre is a real asset** for a product judged by eye — a buyer can stand in a
  finished room before specifying.
- **Track record is real but likely under-documented** (real rooms, the city-named schemes, the Barco
  demo room). Install photography and case studies are a content-phase job; verify before relying on
  any specific install publicly.

## 5. The discipline

Internal throughout — the rules that keep Fabric Walls' positioning honest and un-fenced. This governs
anyone writing Fabric Walls copy.

**Don't let names or origins fence the brand**

- **"Walls" is just the name.** The system treats ceilings and other surfaces too. Never write copy
  that implies walls only.
- **Don't fence to cinema.** Home cinema, media rooms, DCI, luxury living, and commercial fit-out are
  all in scope. Cinema is where it started, not its limit.
- **Acoustics is application-specific — never a universal transparency claim.** Transparent for speakers
  behind the fabric; absorptive (fabric + Acoustic Core) to calm a commercial room. Claiming
  transparency everywhere both misleads and re-fences to cinema.

**Get the value story right**

- **Lead with certainty and time.** The result is settled in the factory; the install is a fraction of
  the time. Keep "less effort / lower skill" in the background — real but secondary, and over-leaning on
  it implies the dealer can't cope.
- **Never imply the dealer can't.** It's a fear-, labour- and time problem, not a skill gap.
- **Sell it as a product, not a box of bits.** It should feel like buying a finished thing that goes up
  predictably — not a DIY kit to figure out. This is what fit-out contractors respond to.
- **Consistency means approach and result, never design.** Never imply cookie-cutter sameness; the look
  is deliberately flexible.

**Mechanics and honesty**

- **Self-serve tools are right here** — calculators and the trade store. This is the deliberate opposite
  of C-ATS; do **not** carry the no-calculator rule across.
- **Proof is visual first** — "you can see it." The lab data covers only the acoustic behaviour you
  can't see.
- **Only claim what's backed** — factory precision, the push-fit silicone edge, lab-tested acoustic
  behaviour. No invented figures.

**Partners, rivals, group**

- **Do name premium fabrics — especially Ultrasuede.** Recognised branded materials sell the product;
  naming them is an ingredient-brand advantage, not a supply-chain leak. (The group rule against naming
  hidden *manufacturing/OEM* partners still holds — a customer-chosen branded fabric is the opposite
  case, and a selling point.)
- **Position against on-site fabric track by value, never by disparagement.**
- **Acoustic design is C-ATS's domain — cross-sell, don't duplicate.** Fabric Walls delivers the
  acoustic behaviour physically, but the design of what a room needs acoustically is C-ATS. Never run a
  competing acoustic-design service (see §6).

## 6. How Fabric Walls fits the group

**Positioning core**

> Fabric Walls is the aesthetic layer of the room — the finished surface every other element integrates
> into. Some things sit behind it: wiring, acoustic treatment, and speakers where the fabric lets their
> sound through. Others are set into it: the screen, the lighting, the ports and vents. Either way, it's
> what turns a collection of separate systems into a single designed space. That makes it a natural way
> into a project, and makes everything else easier to deliver once the surface that unites them is in
> hand.

**The reasoning under it**

- **Fabric Walls is the room's aesthetic layer, not just its cover.** Everything integrates *into* it —
  some elements concealed behind it (treatment, wiring, speakers behind transparent fabric), others set
  into or onto it (screen, port holes, LED, vents, columns). It's the unifying design surface, the
  physical form of the group's whole-room play.
- **Acoustic design stays C-ATS's domain — the alignment rule.** Fabric Walls delivers the acoustic
  *behaviour* physically (transparent, or absorptive via the Acoustic Core), but *what the room needs
  acoustically* is designed by C-ATS. Fabric Walls cross-sells C-ATS for it, never a competing service.
  Fabric Walls executes; C-ATS designs. This removes the overlap the current site implies.
- **It's a strong entry point** because the finished surface is on every room and non-negotiable — a
  fabric or fit-out job opens the door to acoustics (C-ATS), screens and masking (DT), lighting (Light
  Walls) and speakers (Pro-Fi).
- **The commercial push is a group opportunity too** — fit-out where the fabric is the absorber, with
  C-ATS designing the acoustic requirement behind it.
- **The cross-sell only works if the handoff feels like one group** — especially Fabric Walls → C-ATS.
  Seamless, or the whole-room advantage leaks.

## 7. What's still open

Internal only — the unresolved items to settle before they harden into copy.

- **The lead message.** "Faster, smarter fabric interiors" / "easiest, fastest, most reliable" are
  placeholders. Settle the one-line promise — likely built on *certainty and time* (§3).
- **The commercial push beyond cinema.** Moving into commercial fit-out means leading with the
  *absorptive* acoustic story (not transparency) and reaching a different buyer (fit-out contractors).
  How hard to push, and how to reach them, is undecided.
- **Buyer-truth.** The real split between AV integrator and fit-out contractor — who specifies, who
  installs, who pays — from real recent jobs, not invented. Parked, as with C-ATS.
- **The C-ATS acoustic-design overlap — resolved in principle, not yet in execution.** Acoustic design
  is C-ATS's; the Fabric Walls site still lists its own "Acoustic Treatment Design Service." Clean that
  up so the brands align.
- **Interior-Kit level naming.** Levels 1–4 vs Essential/Performance/Advanced — settle one scheme.
- **The Approved Installer programme** — is it real and active, and how does it sit with the group
  partner model?
- **Proof assets.** Install photography and case studies are thin (same gap as C-ATS), despite real
  rooms, the city-named schemes and the Barco demo room. The Experience Centre partly offsets this.
  Content-phase job.
- **Thin reach.** ~85 LinkedIn followers, no YouTube or Instagram. A build task, not an optimisation.
- **The fabric / ingredient-brand story.** Which premium fabrics to name, and how to make Ultrasuede
  work as an ingredient brand.
- **Provenance.** The current site was built ad-hoc; it's a placeholder to re-base, not intent.
