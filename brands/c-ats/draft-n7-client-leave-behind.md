## What this is, and who reads it

**Not our marketing.** *This is a **one-page leave-behind the dealer gives their client** at handover — or earlier,
when the client asks what all the money in the walls bought. **We write it; the dealer delivers it.*** *That makes
it a distinct channel and it needs saying plainly, because `claims.md` `C2.29` excludes the end user from our

**The voice is not the trade voice.** *No coefficients, no test names, no product codes, no jargon. **The client is

## The piece

### Draft title: **The part of the room you cannot see**

**Almost everything in a room like this is visible.** *You can see the screen, the seats, the finishes, the light.
The one thing you cannot see is the part that decides what the room actually **sounds** like — because it is inside
the walls and the ceiling, behind the fabric.*

**It was put there before the walls were finished.** *It had to be. A concealed layer is decided with the wall, not
added to it afterwards — which is why the acoustic design happened early in your project, alongside the drawings
rather than after them.*

### What is in there

**Three different problems, and three different things solving them.**

- **Reflections** — sound bouncing off a hard surface and reaching you a moment after the sound that came straight
  from the speaker. *Left alone, it blurs detail and makes dialogue harder to follow. **The panels handling this
  redirect those reflections away from where you sit** rather than deadening the room.*
- **Resonance** — the deep, uneven bass that some rooms have. *One seat sounds heavy, the next sounds thin. **The
  panels handling this sit in the corners**, where that energy collects.*
- **Reverberation** — the room's overall decay: how long a sound hangs on after it stops. *Too long and everything
  smears together; too short and the room feels dead. **This one is a matter of getting the amount right**, which is
  what the design was for.*

### Why it was designed rather than estimated

**The panels are independently tested**, *so the quantity and the position in your room came from a design, not from
covering the walls until it seemed like enough.*

**And it can be checked.** *Acoustics is usually the one part of a room that nobody can see and nobody can prove.
**This part can be measured** — the room can be tested to confirm it performs as designed. *You do not have to take
it on trust, and neither did the person who specified it.*

### One thing worth knowing about the space

**The treatment is shallow by design.** *Acoustic systems can be very deep, and depth comes out of the room — every
extra millimetre on four walls is floor area you paid for and no longer have. **This system was designed to do the
work in as little depth as possible**, which is why the room is the size it is.*

## Two decisions that are not mine

1. **Should this exist at all, given `C2.29`?** *The end user is excluded from our content audience and B2C runs
   only through the Cinema Store channels. **My reading is that this is different in kind** — it is a tool handed to
   the dealer, not us addressing their client — **and that `../../CLAUDE.md`'s *depth spent on the customer* is the
   rule that governs it.** *But it is the first time this repo has written words intended for an end user's eyes,
   and that deserves to be said out loud rather than slipped through.*
2. **Whose name is on it?** *Three options and they are materially different: **C-ATS-branded** (we are visible in
   the client's house, and the dealer may not want that); **dealer-brandable**, supplied as a template they put
   their own name on (most generous, most likely to be used, and we disappear); or **unbranded**. *The record's own
   scoping test — **"see whether dealers use it"** — probably answers it: the version a dealer will actually hand
   over is the one that makes them look good.* **Not a decision to take by default, so it is stated rather than
   assumed.**

**The scoping test stands as the backlog wrote it:** *one artefact, one brand, then judge. **The measure is not
whether it reads well — it is whether a dealer hands it to anyone.***
