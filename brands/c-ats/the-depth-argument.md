# The depth argument — what it is, why it is true, and how far it may be pushed

**Neil, 2026-08-21: *"the panel depth is the single most important differentiator in the whole system and we need to
ensure that we are 100% clear on what and why on that point. its not just a throw away line."*** *It has been carried
as a bullet in six documents and argued in none. This is the argument.*

**The finding, before the detail: the depth story and the 3 Rs story are the same story**, and the brand has been
telling them as two. The system is **a full-range treatment solution whose panels were designed against the problems
found in small rooms**, and each of the three uses the mechanism that problem actually needs. Depth is what that buys.

*Neil, 2026-08-21 (`../../registers/questions.md` `Q95`): **"we achieve results that are outstanding in an incredibly
shallow depth. in particular this is obvious when comparing the resonance panel to other companies 'bass traps' and the
reflection panel to other companies 'diffusors'."*** **That is the comparison, and it is class against class rather
than company against company** — which is the legitimate form of criticising a category (`../../CLAUDE.md`).

---

## 1. The claim, bounded

**Sanctioned** (`claims.md` `C5.20`, from `Q63`): *"one of the shallowest complete systems on the market."* About the
**whole system**, not a panel.

**Not sanctioned:** the absolute. Neil: *"I do believe it to be the most shallow total system on the market **but that
is not checked** and inevitably such a claim is never 100% certain — certainly 'one of' would be valid!"*

**The figures are stated, not suppressed.** 50 mm for reflection and reverberation, 43 mm for resonance. *An earlier
rule hid them behind "shallower than any competing product"; `Q63` struck it, because the numbers are the premise of
the commercial argument rather than a spec boast.*

**One boundary on "full range".** *It is the owner's description of the design intent and the right one. **The measured
evidence starts at 100 Hz** — BSRIA's lowest third-octave band — so below that the system is designed rather than
verified, and the record notes separately that a 125/250 Hz Sabine RT60 misses the 20–80 Hz modal region entirely
(`measured-acoustics.md`). Say full-range as intent; cite data only from 100 Hz up.*

---

## 2. Why depth is normally needed, and what we do not claim about anyone

**Struck 2026-08-21.** *This section previously explained conventional depth by saying the category uses one mechanism —
porous absorption — for all three problems. **That is a strawman and it is not supportable.** Others build tuned bass
traps and genuine mathematical diffusors; characterising the category as porous-only is an unmeasured claim about
rivals, which is forbidden exactly as overstating ourselves is. Neil: **"you have grossly over simplifed what others do
as well which is just as bad as over stating ourselves."***

**What is true, and is all that is needed:**

**Porous absorption is path-length dependent.** It works by making air move through a resistive material, and the
material has to occupy a meaningful fraction of a wavelength — the working rule of thumb being a quarter wavelength. *At
100 Hz a wavelength is about 3.4 m, so a quarter wavelength is about 860 mm.* **So wherever a problem is addressed by
porous absorption, low frequencies demand depth.** That is general acoustics, and it says nothing about anybody's
product range.

**The two comparisons that are ours to make are between product classes**, and Neil names both:

- **The resonance panel against the category of bass traps.** *A bass trap is whatever a supplier builds to control
  low-frequency energy. The comparison worth making is depth against measured result, and §4 makes it with our own
  data.*
- **The reflection panel against the category of diffusors.** *See §3, because the honest answer there is better than a
  comparison.*

## 3. Why each C-ATS panel can be shallow — one reason each

| Panel | Problem in a small room | Mechanism | Why depth is not the lever |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reflection, 50 mm | Early reflections arriving audibly after the direct sound | **Breaking up the reflection** — surface geometry redirects and disperses it | The work is done by relief and shape, not by bulk. See below |
| Reverberation, 50 mm | Overall decay through the mid and high band | **Porous absorption at high density** — roughly 3.5× standard acoustic foam | Density buys resistance where depth would otherwise have to. It is still path-length dependent, which is why this panel is weak at the bottom — and it does not have to be strong there, because it is not the panel for that job |
| Resonance, 43 mm | Modal, low-frequency energy — the uneven bass of a small room | **A tuned resonator** — a rigid face over a damped cavity, a mass on a spring | A resonator is tuned by mass and cavity, **not** by path length. It is the only class of absorber that works at low frequency in a shallow depth, and it is why 43 mm is possible at all |

**The reflection panel: state the goal, and be honest about the word.** *Neil, `Q95`: **"we know that the ref panel is
not a true mathematical diffusor but that concept is of limited application in a small room anyway. our goal is break
up reflections and move them from audible to inaudible."***

**That is the clearest statement of purpose in the brand and it should lead wherever the panel is described.** *Three
things follow.*

- **The goal is a threshold, not a coefficient.** A reflection does not have to be removed; it has to stop being
  audible as a distinct arrival. That is why the panel's measured absorption is near zero and why that is not a
  weakness — it is not absorbing, and it was never trying to.
- **`C5.15` stops being a constraint and becomes a position.** *The rule was "never say diffusion, because ISO 17497
  makes it a claim we cannot support." **The better version is Neil's**: it is not a mathematical diffusor, and in a
  small room that concept has limited application anyway. **An admission plus a reason beats a silence**, and it lets
  the category's own word be discussed rather than avoided.*
- **It answers `N3` question 4 properly.** *"Why do the absorption figures look low" has been carried as needing an
  explanation of scattering. The real answer is that absorption is the wrong measure for this panel's job.*

**The resonance panel is still the argument's spine.** *Take it away and C-ATS is a shallow mid/high system with the
same low-frequency problem as anything else. `C4.15` is Neil's own words and §4's only row in his voice.*

## 4. The measured proof, from our own report

**BSRIA 100241/1, tested 24 July 2019, BS EN ISO 354:2003, read at source 2026-08-21.** *This is the depth-against-result comparison `Q95` calls for, and it is made **between our own two panels**, so it characterises nobody.* *Same lab, same session, same
1.44 m² coverage, free/unfixed mounting.* **The comparison is between our own two panels, so it names nobody.**

**Table 5, plane absorber at 1.44 m², sound absorption coefficient αs:**

| Frequency | Reverberation panel, 50 mm porous | **Resonance panel, 43 mm resonator** |
|---|---|---|
| 100 Hz | 0.14 | **0.46** |
| 125 Hz | 0.41 | **0.48** |
| 200 Hz | 0.32 | **0.44** |
| 250 Hz | 0.50 | 0.48 |
| 500 Hz | 0.88 | 0.55 |
| 1 kHz | 1.11 | 0.47 |

**At 100 Hz the shallower panel outperforms the deeper one more than three to one.** *And the resonance panel's curve
is flat from 100 Hz to 1 kHz where the porous panel's climbs — which is exactly what a resonator versus a porous
absorber looks like, and it is the physics in §3 showing up in the data.*

**Table 4 gives the corner-loading proof, and it is the more useful number.** *Equivalent absorption area per panel,
m² per object:*

| Configuration | 100 Hz | 125 Hz |
|---|---|---|
| Resonance panel, discrete around the room (the ISO standard arrangement) | 0.11 | 0.19 |
| Resonance panel, **in corners against two walls** | **0.37** | **0.47** |
| Resonance panel, room edges against one wall | 0.20 | 0.35 |
| *Reverberation panel, discrete, for scale* | 0.04 | 0.03 |

**Corner placement more than triples the panel's low-frequency output** — 0.11 to 0.37 at 100 Hz. *BSRIA records that
the corner configuration was **"bespoke and representative of how Cinema Acoustic Treatment Systems Ltd would install
the material in real life"**, so it is our configuration, measured, and it should be cited as such rather than as the
standard result.*

*And the last row is why the reflection and reverberation panels cannot be sold as bass control: at 100 Hz the porous
panel returns 0.04 m² per object. The resonance panel corner-loaded returns 0.37. **Nine times, at the same depth
class.***

**One outside reference, and it compares against a raw material rather than a product.** *Our own foam's manufacturer publishes its
absorption at four thicknesses. At **100 mm** — twice the depth of anything C-ATS ships — that porous foam reaches
**0.41 at 125 Hz**. The 43 mm resonance panel reaches **0.48**. **A 43 mm panel beating a 100 mm porous absorber at
125 Hz is the depth argument in one line**, and it names no competitor because the comparison is against a raw
material.* **Caveat that must travel with it:** *different lab, different session, and the older BS EN 20354
designation of the same reverberation-room method — so it is indicative rather than a controlled comparison, and must
be presented that way.*

## 5. Why depth is worth money, and it is not a small amount

**The treatment sets the wall build-up.** *Neil, `Q92`: the panels sit in the cavity between the structural wall and
the fabric wall, and **"the depth is not driven by the fabric system, its driven by the acoustic treatment system which
is entirely the point of making it so shallow."*** *So this is not "we fit inside a zone you were giving up anyway" —
it is the dimension the architect is being asked to give up, and we decide it.*

**On a 6.0 × 4.5 m room**, a 100 mm reduction in build-up on all four sides returns about **2 m²** — and at prime UK
values that is a five-figure sum, larger than the treatment that would have consumed it. *The figures are on the
published depth article; **they are not repeated here and must not travel into trade material**, because the money
argument is a consumer and end-client argument and the trade reader is buying floor area, not being sold a headline.*

**`C1.9` states the mechanism of the argument** — *the floor area a deeper system consumes costs more than the
treatment itself* — and it is the only claim row in this area that is a business argument rather than a spec.

## 7. What is still missing

- **The absolute is unchecked.** *A survey of comparable complete systems' total depths would settle it. Until then the
  bounded form is the only honest one — and it is not weak.*
- **Below 100 Hz is designed, not verified.** *BSRIA's lowest band is 100 Hz and a small room's worst modal trouble sits
  under it. **This is the gap most worth closing**, because it is the exact region the resonance panel exists for and
  the region "full range" is understood to include. It is also what `KNW-4` decided to defer on cost — and now has a
  specific target rather than a general one. `DAT-5`.*
- **The resonator's tuning is not recorded.** *Nothing states a centre frequency, a range or a Q. The measured curve
  implies a broad, well-damped response — flat from 100 Hz to 1 kHz — but that is read off results rather than stated by
  whoever designed it. **A broadly tuned resonator is a much better story than a narrow one**, and the strongest claim
  in the range currently rests on an inference. `KNW-13`.*
- **The 100–200 mm characterisation of conventional practice is unsourced**, and after `Q95` it needs handling with
  more care than a citation: *it must describe **what porous absorption requires**, not what competitors build.*
  `DAT-4`.
- **"Audible to inaudible" has no threshold attached.** *The goal is now stated properly and it is a threshold claim, so
  the obvious next question is: below what? There is established work on the audibility of early reflections, and if the
  design targets a figure, that figure is the strongest single number the reflection panel could carry. `KNW-14`.*
