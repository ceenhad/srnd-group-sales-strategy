# The depth argument — what it is, why it is true, and how far it may be pushed

**Neil, 2026-08-21: *"the panel depth is the single most important differentiator in the whole system and we need to
ensure that we are 100% clear on what and why on that point. its not just a throw away line."*** *It has been carried
as a bullet in six documents and argued in none. This is the argument.*

**The finding, before the detail: the depth story and the 3 Rs story are the same story**, and the brand has been
telling them as two. Conventional treatment is deep because it uses **one mechanism for three problems**. C-ATS is
shallow because it uses **the right mechanism for each**, and only one of the three needs depth.

---

## 1. The claim, bounded

**Sanctioned** (`claims.md` `C5.20`, from `Q63`): *"one of the shallowest complete systems on the market."* About the
**whole system**, not a panel.

**Not sanctioned:** the absolute. Neil: *"I do believe it to be the most shallow total system on the market **but that
is not checked** and inevitably such a claim is never 100% certain — certainly 'one of' would be valid!"*

**The figures are stated, not suppressed.** 50 mm for reflection and reverberation, 43 mm for resonance. *An earlier
rule hid them behind "shallower than any competing product"; `Q63` struck it, because the numbers are the premise of
the commercial argument rather than a spec boast.*

---

## 2. Why a conventional system is deep

**Porous absorption is the default mechanism, and it is path-length dependent.** A porous absorber works by making air
move through a resistive material; the material has to occupy a meaningful fraction of a wavelength to do it. The
working rule of thumb is a quarter wavelength.

*At 100 Hz a wavelength is about 3.4 m, so a quarter wavelength is about 860 mm.* **No practical porous absorber is
deep enough to work at the bottom of the range.** That is why conventional treatment lands at 100–200 mm: it is a
compromise, chasing bass with a mechanism that cannot reach it, and the depth is bought to buy performance it never
quite gets.

**This is general acoustics, not a C-ATS measurement**, and it should be stated as such. It is also why the honest
version of the argument is about mechanism rather than about our cleverness.

---

## 3. Why each C-ATS panel can be shallow — one reason each

| Panel | Problem | Mechanism | Why depth is not the lever |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reflection, 50 mm | Early reflections off hard surfaces | **Scattering.** Surface geometry redirects the reflection | A scatterer works by relief and shape, not by bulk. It needs a moulded face, not a thick one |
| Reverberation, 50 mm | Overall decay, mid and high frequency | **Porous absorption, but at high density** — 85 kg/m³, roughly 3.5× standard acoustic foam | Density buys resistance where depth would otherwise have to. It is still path-length dependent, which is why this panel is the one that is weak at the bottom — and it does not have to be strong there, because it is not the panel for that job |
| Resonance, 43 mm | Modal, low-frequency energy | **A tuned resonator** — a rigid face over a damped cavity, a mass on a spring | A resonator is tuned by mass and cavity, **not** by path length. It is the only class of absorber that works at low frequency in a shallow depth, and it is why 43 mm is possible at all |

**The resonance panel is the whole argument.** *Take it away and C-ATS is a shallow mid/high system with the same bass
problem as everyone else. `C4.15` is Neil's own words on it and it is §4's only row in his voice: **"a very, very
unusual product delivering results much deeper panels would be proud of."***

## 4. The measured proof, from our own report

**BSRIA 100241/1, tested 24 July 2019, BS EN ISO 354:2003, read at source 2026-08-21.** *Same lab, same session, same
1.44 m² coverage, free/unfixed mounting.* **The comparison is between our own two panels, so it names nobody.**

**Table 5, plane absorber at 1.44 m², sound absorption coefficient αs:**

| Frequency | Reverberation panel, 50 mm porous | **Resonance panel, 43 mm resonator** |
|---|---|---|
| 100 Hz | 0.14 | **0.46** |
| 125 Hz | 0.41 | **0.48** |
| 200 Hz | 0.32 | **0.44** |
| 250 Hz | 0.50 | 0.48 |
| 500 Hz | 0.88 | 0.55 |
| 1 kHz | 1.11 | 0.47 |

**At 100 Hz the shallower panel outperforms the deeper one more than three to one.** *And the resonance panel's curve
is flat from 100 Hz to 1 kHz where the porous panel's climbs — which is exactly what a resonator versus a porous
absorber looks like, and it is the physics in §3 showing up in the data.*

**Table 4 gives the corner-loading proof, and it is the more useful number.** *Equivalent absorption area per panel,
m² per object:*

| Configuration | 100 Hz | 125 Hz |
|---|---|---|
| Resonance panel, discrete around the room (the ISO standard arrangement) | 0.11 | 0.19 |
| Resonance panel, **in corners against two walls** | **0.37** | **0.47** |
| Resonance panel, room edges against one wall | 0.20 | 0.35 |
| *Reverberation panel, discrete, for scale* | 0.04 | 0.03 |

**Corner placement more than triples the panel's low-frequency output** — 0.11 to 0.37 at 100 Hz. *BSRIA records that
the corner configuration was **"bespoke and representative of how Cinema Acoustic Treatment Systems Ltd would install
the material in real life"**, so it is our configuration, measured, and it should be cited as such rather than as the
standard result.*

*And the last row is why the reflection and reverberation panels cannot be sold as bass control: at 100 Hz the porous
panel returns 0.04 m² per object. The resonance panel corner-loaded returns 0.37. **Nine times, at the same depth
class.***

**One outside reference, and it is the strongest thing available.** *Our own foam's manufacturer publishes its
absorption at four thicknesses. At **100 mm** — twice the depth of anything C-ATS ships — that porous foam reaches
**0.41 at 125 Hz**. The 43 mm resonance panel reaches **0.48**. **A 43 mm panel beating a 100 mm porous absorber at
125 Hz is the depth argument in one line**, and it names no competitor because the comparison is against a raw
material.* **Caveat that must travel with it:** *different lab, different session, and the older BS EN 20354
designation of the same reverberation-room method — so it is indicative rather than a controlled comparison, and must
be presented that way.*

## 5. Why depth is worth money, and it is not a small amount

**The treatment sets the wall build-up.** *Neil, `Q92`: the panels sit in the cavity between the structural wall and
the fabric wall, and **"the depth is not driven by the fabric system, its driven by the acoustic treatment system which
is entirely the point of making it so shallow."*** *So this is not "we fit inside a zone you were giving up anyway" —
it is the dimension the architect is being asked to give up, and we decide it.*

**On a 6.0 × 4.5 m room**, a 100 mm reduction in build-up on all four sides returns about **2 m²** — and at prime UK
values that is a five-figure sum, larger than the treatment that would have consumed it. *The figures are on the
published depth article; **they are not repeated here and must not travel into trade material**, because the money
argument is a consumer and end-client argument and the trade reader is buying floor area, not being sold a headline.*

**`C1.9` states the mechanism of the argument** — *the floor area a deeper system consumes costs more than the
treatment itself* — and it is the only claim row in this area that is a business argument rather than a spec.

## 7. What is still missing, and one of them is cheap

- **The absolute is unchecked.** *It would take a survey of comparable complete systems' total depths. Until then the
  bounded form is the only honest one — and the bounded form is not weak.*
- **No measured competitor comparison exists**, and under `CLAUDE.md` no competitor may be named publicly anyway. *The
  two comparisons in §4 are the substitute and they are better than they look, because both are honest.*
- **The resonator's tuning is not recorded.** *Nothing in this repo states what frequency the resonance panel is tuned
  to, or over what range. **The measured curve implies a broad, well-damped response rather than a sharp one** — flat
  from 100 Hz to 1 kHz — but that is read off a graph of results rather than stated by the designer. `KNW-13`.*
- **The 100–200 mm characterisation of conventional practice is unsourced.** *It is used in published content. It is
  almost certainly fair, and it is not evidenced in this repo. `DAT-4`.*
