# C-ATS — the three direct sequences, touch by touch

*What the sequences actually look like (plan items 49–51; S26, `../../motion/motion-design.md`). Every touch carries value
on its own; the ask rides along, never as the payload. Every touch is built from material that already
exists or is already planned — nothing invented, nothing promised that isn't built (no training claims, no
discounts: the depth is spent, never the margin). **Timings are proposals for correction.** Triggers marked
⚑ have no automatic signal yet and are a person's judgement until engine carries them.*

---

## Sequence A — the welcome (state: registered, no order · tempting G4)

*Retimed 2026-08-02 per S28: communication has no start and no end — project lead times run to a year, so
the welcome opens a relationship and **merges into the continuous per-brand stream**; it never goes silent.
The campaign cap holds: no more than three mails in two weeks unless transactional (A1 is transactional).
Switches to sequence B the moment an order lands.*

| # | When | The touch | The value it carries | The ask, riding along |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | On registration *(transactional)* | **"Your account, plainly."** What it grants today — trade pricing now visible, the store, and who to ask by name | Orientation, honestly stated — no benefits that aren't built | Browse the range |
| A2 | ~Week 1 | **The proof, handed over.** The BSRIA report and the how-to-read-an-absorption-table article | Specifier-grade third-party data they can reuse in their own client conversations | The panel pages |
| A3 | ~Week 2 | **"Spec your first room."** The two routes — specify from the documentation, or take the design service, their choice — with the three reference room layouts | A shortcut to a real spec | The entry order, or a Zoom |
| A4 | ~Week 4 | **What the good installers know.** The two critical install notes (bond-vs-screw; the warm-room rule) | The two predictable failures, avoided before they've bought | Quiet — the product pages |
| A5 | ~Week 6 | **A named person.** A short note from whoever owns the relationship: how we work, what they can help with, book a call if useful | A human, before they need one | The Zoom booking |
| — | From there | **Merges into the brand's monthly partner stream** (sequence C3) — no end, no silence | A coin at the brand rhythm, indefinitely | Presence |
| — | On a signal ⚑ | **A person asks** — judgement, not a calendar; a year to first order is normal in this trade | — | The order |

## Sequence B — the install pack and check-in (state: ordered, job ahead · tempting G5)

*Starts when the order is placed. Every touch exists to make the first job go right — the first order tests
us, not the product.*

| # | When | The touch | The value it carries | The ask, riding along |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | With order confirmation | **The install pack, by email.** The ordered panels' instruction pages, the site-conditions paragraph (the 18 °C rule where REV-CP is in the order), scope of supply — what's in the box | The job planned right before the box arrives | "Conditions ready before you fit" |
| B2 | In the box | **The one-page sheet** for each panel type ordered — the critical note in print, where the installer actually is | The answer at the moment of need | The support route, printed |
| B3 | ~3 days after delivery ⚑ | **The check-in.** Arrived intact? Room conditions ready? — with the fault-finding link and the verification offer | Support offered before it's needed, not after it's gone wrong | "Tell us how it goes" |
| B4 | After the job ⚑ | **"How did it go?"** — and, where the client permits, the offer to photograph the finished room professionally at our cost | Recognition and a usable reference for *them* | The three lines we capture (project, unsure, afraid) — our discovery, their support |

## Sequence C — the adjacency stream (state: first job done · tempting G6 → Fabric Walls stage 2)

*Not a drip on a calendar — touches keyed to what they actually bought and did. This is the loop's
machinery: a C-ATS account being walked, gently and usefully, to the next layer of the same room.*

| # | When | The touch | The value it carries | The ask, riding along |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | After the job completes ⚑ | **"The treatment disappears."** The concealment answer — what goes over the panels they just fitted (the staged KB article that already routes to Fabric Walls) | The finishing answer to the room they just built | Fabric Walls' stage 2 — the factory-vs-site film |
| C2 | In the next spec conversation | **The adjacency prompt — spoken, not sent.** When helping spec the acoustics, the fabric and front-of-room structure are the natural next questions (the XS-1 map, used live) | Help with the room they're actually building | The next layer, at the moment it's decided |
| C3 | **~Monthly, per brand** (S29) | **The partner note.** One genuinely useful piece — a newly published answer, a mechanism film. Never news about us; always useful to them. **Subscribed per brand**: partners choose what they receive and can silence what they don't | A coin every time, whether or not they're buying | None overt — presence |
| C4 | When a reorder is natural ⚑ | **Reorder ease.** "The same spec again is one order" — practical, short | Their time | The repeat order |

*Off-sequence, deliberately: the appreciation acts. Recognition is personal and never automated
(`../../group-strategy/partner-programme.md`) — it would stop being a coin the moment it became a template.*

## Notes for the truth-check

- **A5 and the check-ins assume a named person's time** — the one recurring human cost in the three
  sequences. Whose name is deliberately not written here: nothing is formally decided about who does what.
- **B4's three-line capture is the discovery mechanism** (XS-2) arriving through a sequence rather than a
  form — the same act serves them and informs us.
- **C3's rhythm is set: ~monthly per brand (S29)** — which across brands already exceeds one a week
  group-wide, hence per-brand subscription management as a required mechanism.
- **Dependencies:** A2 needs EST-5 (the proof surfaced); A3 needs EST-6 (layouts deployed); A4/B1/B2 need
  DOC-1 published; C1 needs the KB live; C2 needs XS-1 written. The sequences are assembly — every touch
  already exists somewhere in the plan.
