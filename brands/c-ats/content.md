# C-ATS — the content across the buyer journey

This is C-ATS's version of the content that feeds the group buyer journey
(`../../group/02-buyer-journey.md`). The 3 Rs is the core of the content. It's the engineering
brief, not a marketing device, and it's exactly the plain one-problem-one-panel logic a practical,
technical buyer likes. The layered build below is C-ATS's application of the group intro-campaign
template (`../../group/04-content.md`) — that's where the phase structure, the text-forward
principle and the Aphex Twin case study live; this file is the C-ATS-specific instance. These layers
feed the early stages of the journey; they are content, not a standalone campaign.

**Why lead with the problem, not the product.** C-ATS's panels are **designed to be hidden**, so
there is no finished-room photo that shows the product at all. The hook can't lead with imagery of
the thing being sold — it has to lead with the problem being solved (the 3 Rs) and the constraint
(shallow depth in a room that can't spare it).

## Guardrails — every brand `CLAUDE.md` non-negotiable applies hardest in hook copy

Where there's least room to caveat, these bind hardest:
- **scattering, never diffusion;**
- panels named by problem, never mechanism ("Reflection Control Panel," never "diffuser");
- no invented performance figures;
- no supplier names;
- no NDA install details;
- don't tease the commercial range;
- don't hard-code the "Complete" name expansion.

## Creative approach — text-forward, not room-forward

Bold, short statements carrying the 3 Rs claim, over a close, honest crop of the real product (panel
face, corner mid-install) rather than a styled room shot that isn't ours to claim (that's the fabric
finisher's beauty). When the imagery can't differentiate, the words must — bold and specific enough
to stand alone on a feed or a magazine page. Illustrative only, needs creative refinement:
- *"3 acoustic problems. 1 system. 50mm."*
- *"Reflection. Reverberation. Resonance. One panel for each."*
- *"Cinema acoustics that don't cost you the room."*

## The layers (each feeds a buyer-journey stage)

- **Hook (stage 1).** Two strands: (1) the problem named — three problems, one system, one panel
  each; (2) the constraint solved — shallow depth. Absorbable in a single image/statement. Formats:
  graphical layouts per strand, a few colourways, per-platform cuts, a short (~15s) video.
- **Range (stage 2).** What's possible: a data card per panel (REF-CP, RES-CP, REV-CP-50) carrying
  its measured headline figure; an install/checkerboard-placement video; short social/email copy.
  Converges with the pre-planned reference layouts (see the C-ATS site repo) — those rooms are the
  "range in context" asset once shipped.
- **How it works (stage 3).** Mostly repackaging, not new production — the existing KB articles
  (RT60, first reflections, panel thickness, fire ratings…) already cover this. Add
  room-construction breakdowns and the measured data sheet itself; longer-form blog/email.
- **Consolidate (stage 6 / ongoing).** Bring it together into evergreen trade collateral — a
  brochure of products and services. This is the collateral gap; it's where that gap gets filled,
  and what a dealer (or the group) hands over.

Content re-pointed per channel (`growth-levers.md`, lever 2) reuses this core — the 3 Rs holds for
pro install, commercial cinema and fit-out; what changes is the proof emphasis (raw data vs install
ease vs volume-at-scale) and the objection each channel raises.

> **Group vs brand.** The layered-build *structure* (hook → range → how-it-works → consolidate,
> mapped to buyer-journey stages) is a reusable content pattern that could move up to group as a
> template. The 3 Rs core and all copy here are C-ATS's.
