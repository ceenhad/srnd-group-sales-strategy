## Why am I here, and what will I learn

*The learning-objectives frame the CPD assessor asks for, and the `T2` draft was missing.*

You will leave able to answer three questions on any project: **which fire regime this job is under**, **what the
inspector will actually look at**, and **what to ask us for, when**.

You will not leave with a certificate in your hand. Certificates are issued against a specification, and the whole
point of this session is that a certificate for "the panel" is not a thing that exists.

---

## 1. Three regimes, and a job is only ever under one of them

| Regime | Where it applies | What it classifies |
|---|---|---|
| **Class 0** | UK building work, wall and ceiling linings | Surface spread of flame under BS 476 Part 7, **plus** a fire propagation limit under BS 476 Part 6. Both, or it is not Class 0 |
| **EN 13501-1** | European specifications, and increasingly UK ones written to Euroclass | A1 to F, with a smoke sub-rating `s1`–`s3` and a flaming-droplet sub-rating `d0`–`d2`. **A Euroclass without both suffixes is an incomplete answer** |
| **IMO FTP Code** | Vessels. In practice, a **Lloyd's Register** sign-off | **Annex 1 Part 2** — smoke and toxicity. **Annex 1 Part 5** — surface flammability. Those two, named |

**The first thing to get right on a project is which column you are in**, because the three do not convert. A Class 0
line on a datasheet does not answer a Euroclass question, and neither answers a marine surveyor.

*If you take one thing from this table: on a yacht job, ask for Annex 1 Parts 2 and 5 by name. "IMO/SOLAS compliant"
is not a specification and a surveyor will not accept it as one.*

---

## 2. A classification belongs to a material or an assembly. It never belongs to a product name

This is the part that costs dealers time, and it is not a C-ATS quirk — it is how the standards work.

A reaction-to-fire class is issued against **a tested material in a stated build-up**. Change the substrate, the
adhesive, the cavity or the facing and you are outside the certificate. That is why a datasheet says what the core is
classified as rather than what the product is classified as, and why any honest supplier tells you to confirm the
certificate against **your** specification rather than quoting a general class at you.

**So the useful question is never "is this panel fire rated".** It is: *what is each layer, what is each layer
classified as, and what is the surface the room actually presents?*

---

## 3. In a C-ATS room, the surface the inspector looks at is the fabric

**The panels are concealed.** They sit behind a stretch-fabric wall or ceiling system, which is the finished face. So
in the completed room, **the exposed lining surface is the fabric, not our panel** — and the fabric carries its own
reaction-to-fire classification, specified and certified by whoever supplies it.

*This is the single most useful thing in this session.* The reflex is to chase the treatment's rating, and on a
concealed system that is the wrong document to chase first. Two consequences on a live job:

- **Specify the fabric's rating deliberately, at the same time as the fabric.** It is the one that appears on the
  inspector's list, and it is the one most often left to whoever happens to be supplying the finish.
- **The treatment behind it is still specified**, because a concealed layer is part of the construction and a
  specification may still call for its classification. But it is the second question, not the first.

---

## 4. What C-ATS holds, honestly

*The range is four different materials. There is no single answer, and anyone who gives you one is guessing.*

| Layer | What it is | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|
| Reverberation panel core | An acoustic foam core | **Class 0** — Class 1 surface spread under BS 476 Part 7, with the Part 6 propagation limit. The strongest position in the range |
| Reflection panel | An injection-moulded flame-retardant polymer | **UL 94 V-0** on the manufacturer's data. A useful rating, and **not** a UK or European building classification |
| Resonance panel, rear | An acoustic foam | Combustion-modified, BS 3379 nominal class A, compliant with the Furniture and Furnishings Regulations Schedule 1 Part 1, capable of UL 94 HF1 and FMVSS 302 |
| Resonance panel, front | A cast acrylic sheet | See the decision at the foot of this draft |
| Marine reflection panel | A solid-surface material, **specified by Lloyd's Register** | The material's own marine certificate is the evidence, and it is what a surveyor is given |

**Two limits stated plainly, because a partial fire answer is the one that gets a dealer refused on site.**

- **There is no EN 13501-1 classification for any C-ATS panel.** If a specification is written to Euroclass, we cannot
  answer it from our own data today. Ask early, so the specification can be written to what exists or the
  classification can be obtained.
- **There is no panel-level marine fire test.** For marine, the evidence is the material's certificate — which is the
  normal basis for marine linings, and the material was Lloyd's own choice.

---

## 5. How to get the right document

1. **Establish the regime first** — Building Regs, Euroclass, or Lloyd's. One of the three.
2. **List the layers**, including the fabric and the adhesive. The specification is the assembly, not the panel.
3. **Ask us for the certificate against that specification**, naming the products and the finish. We supply on a
   project basis; there is no download, and that is deliberate.
4. **Ask early.** A certificate request answered at tender stage is administration. The same request answered the week
   before an inspection is a programme problem.

## The one decision

**Does the resonance panel's cast acrylic front face go in the piece, with its Class 3 surface-spread rating and its
manufacturer's own words that it is combustible?**

*The case for including it:* it is true, a dealer who is blindsided by it on site is worse off than one who was told,
and the mitigation is strong and easy to state — the face is concealed behind a rated fabric, and the acrylic's own
manufacturer notes that it produces very little smoke, which is the property a lining regime cares most about.
Including it is also the difference between a compliance session and a sales session, and the CPD routes assess for
exactly that.

*The case against:* it is the weakest number in the range, stated publicly, in a piece whose purpose is to build
confidence. It could instead be answered honestly on a project basis, as everything else is.

**My reading: include it.** *A compliance subject that omits its own weak point is not a compliance subject, and the
concealment argument is genuinely good rather than a deflection. But this is a brand-truth call and it is Neil's.*
