## The piece

**Audience:** the integrator, on site or planning the install.
**Length:** one page, plus a short recording of the same thing.
**Its one job:** *remove the fear that acoustic treatment needs set-out precision.*

### The claim

**Reflection and reverberation panels can sit up to about 300 mm away from their designed position without
materially changing the result.**

*So the install does not need a survey, a laser line or a designer on site to check it. Get the panel on the right
surface, in the right area, and the room performs as designed.*

### Why it is true, in the dealer's terms

**The acoustics do not depend on set-out precision.** *What matters is **which surface** a panel is on and roughly
**where on it** — because the mechanism is a surface interacting with sound arriving over a wide area, not a device
aimed at a point. **Move a panel 300 mm along a wall and it is still doing the same job to the same surface.***

### The two limits — and stating them is the point

**1. The resonance panel is the exception, and it is not a small one.** *The corner panel's effectiveness **depends
on corner placement** — away from a corner it is a much weaker device. **This is the one product in the range where
position is not forgiving.*** *(`product-records.md` `O2`, resonance record.)*

**2. There is a height rule that the tolerance does not override.** *The lowest panel in a block should sit **no
higher than 30% of room height** off the floor, and rooms over 3 m tall need proportionally more.*
*(`product-records.md`, reflection record, `Limits`.)*

### Why we say this out loud when competitors don't

**Because it is the dealer's advantage, not ours.** *A forgiving layout is what lets an integrator install acoustic
treatment **without an acoustician on site** — which is the whole C-ATS proposition stated as a practical fact
rather than a claim (`claims.md` `C1.39`: **a system a dealer runs without hiring the author**).*

**And it is the strongest available evidence for that proposition**, because it is checkable on the first job. *The
tier that competes on rooms sells designs that need their people in them; a ±300 mm tolerance is the opposite of
that, and it costs a dealer nothing to verify.*

**⚠ Confirm before publication — two items, and the first is the whole piece.**

1. **The ~300 mm figure traces to a decode of a 2015 pack brochure**, and the provenance table flags that source as
   **"legacy, confirm current spec"** (`product-records.md` § Provenance). *It is very probably still right — but
   this piece is **entirely** that number, so it is the one thing that must be confirmed rather than assumed.*
   **Old is unestablished, not wrong; it still has to be established before it is taught.**
2. **The tolerance is stated as "~300 mm" everywhere.** *Publishing it as an exact figure would be a precision the
   record does not claim. **Keep the approximation** — and "about 300 mm" is more credible to an installer than
   "300 mm" anyway.*

**What this piece deliberately does not do** *(`PR-4`, and Neil 2026-08-19: "if it's not legal or safety no one
gives a shit — hardly any dealer cares about Sabines")*: **it quotes no coefficient, no test standard and no
measurement.** *The tolerance is a fact about the dealer's working day. That is why it is the subject.*
