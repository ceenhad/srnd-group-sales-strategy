## The answers

### 3 · Can the panels go behind fabric?

**Yes — that is how they are meant to be installed.** *All three panels sit behind a stretch-fabric finish,
concealed, and any stretch-fabric system works. **Fabric Walls pairs naturally with them and is not a
requirement.*** *They also work behind an acoustically transparent projection screen, which puts the image surface,
the speakers and the acoustic treatment in one wall.*

**The thing worth knowing is the consequence, not the compatibility:** *because the treatment is concealed, **it has
to be decided before the wall that conceals it is built.*** *(That is its own subject —
`draft-t2-decide-before-the-wall.md`.)*

### 4 · Why do the absorption figures look low?

**Because the reflection panel is not an absorber, and absorption is the wrong measure for it.** *It **scatters** —
it redirects reflected energy away from the listening position. A device that redirects sound is supposed to show
little absorption; **a high absorption figure on that panel would mean it was doing the wrong job.***

**And there are two published curves, not one number.** *The chart carries two series because **the panel performs
differently depending on how it is fixed** — bonded and screw-fixed are acoustically different, and both have been
published for years. **Our designs use the bonded figures as the default** (Neil, `Q46`).*

*If a dealer wants the number that matters: it is the one for the panel doing the job it exists for, in the
configuration it is actually installed in. **The comparison to refuse is a scatterer against an absorber.***

> **⚠ Publication note.** *This answer quotes no coefficients, deliberately. **`DOC-4` is still open** — the exact
> plotted A and B values have not been read off the published chart — so **the explanation may publish and the
> table may not.*** *And the word is **scattering**: the legacy pack brochure calls this panel a "faceted ABS
> diffuser" and that wording is wrong and must not be reproduced (`claims.md` `C5.15`).*

### 7 · Why is the resonance panel bigger than the others?

**Because the corner is where the work is, and the work needs area.** *A low-frequency absorber placed in a corner
is sitting where the room's bass pressure is highest — that is what lets a **43 mm** panel do a job that would
otherwise need a far deeper device. **But the mechanism scales with the area you present to that pressure**, so the
panel is deliberately the larger format.*

**The trade the dealer is actually being offered:** *area on a corner, instead of depth into the room. **In a
high-end room the floor area a deep system consumes costs considerably more than the treatment does*** (Neil,
2026-08-19). *That is why the panel is the shape it is.*

### 8 · Can the resonance panel go anywhere other than a corner?

**It can be fixed anywhere. It will not do the same job.** *Its effectiveness depends on corner placement —
**away from a corner it is a much weaker device**, because the thing it exploits is only there in the corner.*

**This is worth answering plainly rather than softening**, *because it is the one place in the range where position
is not forgiving. Everywhere else the layout tolerates being approximate (`draft-t5-layout-tolerance.md`); **the
corner panel does not, and a dealer is better served knowing which rule is which.***

### 10 · Checkerboard the reverberation panels, or run them continuous?

**Checkerboard, weighted toward the rear of the room.** *The broadband panel works above roughly 200 Hz across
wide areas of wall and ceiling, and **spread coverage does more than a solid block of the same quantity** — which
is also why it is worth putting more of it behind the seats than in front of them.*

**And the limit that belongs with the answer:** *over-use dulls a room. **That is a design question, not a product
limit** — more is not better past the target, and the point of designing the room is knowing where that is.*

> **⚠ Gate to check before this publishes.** *Coverage **quantity** is on `DR-Q52`'s boundary and stays out of
> this answer. **Pattern is stated here as intended use**, from the product record, not from the design rules — but
> confirm that reading holds before it goes out.*
