## The piece

**Audience:** the architect and the interior designer first, the integrator alongside them.
**Length:** a 45-minute seminar, or a two-page note. *The seminar is the point — see the route note below.*
**Its one job:** *move the acoustic decision out of the equipment stage and into the building stage, by showing that
it is a **construction** decision, not a kit decision.*

### The claim

**By the time the wall is finished, the acoustic decision has already been made — including the decision not to
treat.** *Acoustic treatment in a high-end room is concealed. It sits behind the finish, inside the wall build-up.
So it is specified with the wall, not after it.*

### Four reasons it sits earlier in the programme than people expect

**1. It is inside the wall, not on it.** *The panels sit behind a stretch-fabric finish, concealed — that is how
rooms at this level are finished. **A concealed layer has to be decided before the layer that conceals it.*** Once
the fabric is on, changing the acoustics means opening the wall again.

**2. It changes the finished dimensions of the room.** *The treatment depth plus the fabric layer is part of the
build-out from the structural wall — so **the internal dimensions on the drawing depend on which treatment is
inside it.** A deeper system takes more of the room. **At this level the floor area a deep system consumes costs
considerably more than the treatment itself**, which is why minimum depth was a design goal of this system rather
than a specification detail (Neil, 2026-08-19).* **This is the reason the subject belongs to the architect and not
only to the AV budget.**

**3. The corners have to still be available.** *Low-frequency control is a **corner** problem — the absorber is
corner-loaded, and it needs a corner to work in. Corners are also where boxing, services and speakers end up. **If
the corners are committed before anyone has looked at the acoustics, the one element of the system whose position is
not negotiable has nowhere to go.***

**4. The acoustic design is drawn *from* the specifier's documents, not after them.** *Neil, 2026-08-19: **"you need
full data on the room finishes etc. to do an acoustic design — that's not C-ATS specific."** The inputs are the
finish schedule, the geometry and the seating — the architect's and the interior designer's own drawings. **So the
acoustic design is not a thing that happens once the room is chosen; it is a thing that reads the choices.***

*And one programme item for the installer rather than the specifier: **the broadband panel's adhesive needs the room
conditioned above 18 °C for 24 hours before it goes on.** A site-readiness line, not a product limitation — but it
is on the programme, not in the box.*

### What is actually lost by leaving it late — and what we do not know

**What becomes unavailable is derivable from the four points above, and it is worth stating plainly:** *the wall has
to be reopened; the dimensions on the approved drawings are already fixed; the corners are already committed. **Each
of those is a cost carried by the client for a decision nobody made deliberately.***

**What we do not know — and the piece must not pretend to:** *when the acoustic decision **actually** gets made on a
real job. `product-records.md` `N6` marks it `[?]`.* **So the piece argues from what the construction sequence
requires, never from a claimed rate of failure.** *No "most projects leave it too late". We have not counted, and an
audience of architects is exactly the audience who would ask how we know.*

### Why this is a class and not a pitch

**Three quarters of the argument above is true of any concealed acoustic treatment, not only ours.** *That is not a
weakness of the piece — it is what makes it teachable. **The subject is the sequence, and the sequence is a fact
about buildings.***

**C-ATS enters at reason 2 and nowhere else.** *If the depth of the treatment is part of the wall build-up, then a
shallower system gives the room back — and that is a comparison a specifier can make for themselves, from published
dimensions, without us asserting anything.* **Lead with the sequence; let the depth follow from it.** *(`../../CLAUDE.md`:
never lead with our own brilliance — and the Scandinavian register, restraint over superlatives.)*

**Who is the hero of the sentence.** *The dealer relationship is still the asset; what widens here is **who is
holding the page**. In a room where the architect and the integrator are both sitting, the win being described is
still the integrator's — they are the one who gets asked for the acoustic spec and can now answer it at the stage
the question is really being asked (`C2.26`).*

## What this deliberately does not do

- **No coefficients, no test names, no measured figures.** *`PR-4`, and Neil 2026-08-19: "if it's not legal or
  safety no one gives a shit."* **The argument is a sequence argument; measurement would weaken it by changing the
  subject.**
- **Nothing from the design rules.** *No placement rule beyond "a corner absorber needs a corner" — **corner-straddle
  placement and the ceiling-corner lever are on `DR-Q52`'s proposed-publishable side and are not decided** (that is
  `T4`, and it stays gated).*
- **No room type.** *The subject is the wall build-up, not the cinema. **Cinema credibility can lead the
  introduction; it must not define the scope*** (`../../CLAUDE.md`).
- **No competitor named**, and no unmeasured comparison. *"A shallower system gives the room back" is stated as an
  arithmetic the specifier does themselves from published dimensions.*
