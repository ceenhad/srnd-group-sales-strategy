# Display Technologies (DT) — brand strategy

*Phase-1 clear strategy for DT: the settled positioning that content development and deployment build
against. This document is **internal** — nothing here is website copy as it stands. Each section has a
**positioning core**, written so it could become public copy, and the **internal reasoning** under it.
Grounded in `displaytechnologies.co.uk` (the product line is catalogued in `product-line.md`) and the
ISE 2023 Screen Wall video.*

*DT's strategy is different in kind from C-ATS and Fabric Walls: its historic core market —
residential projection — is shifting under it, so this is as much about **forward direction** as
current positioning. The identity is deliberately set above any single display technology.*

---

## 1. What DT is

**Positioning core**

> Display Technologies makes solutions for the architectural integration of technology. In a high-end
> room, the picture, the sound and the equipment that create them have to serve the experience without
> dominating the space — so DT designs and builds the screens, the masking, the mounts and concealment,
> and the modular front wall that let all of it integrate into the architecture. Each is a
> precision-engineered product with the control built inside it, made to the size of the equipment and
> the room, and made to carry whatever creates the picture — projector or LED.

**The reasoning under it**

- **The identity is the architectural integration of technology, not projection.** The manufacturing
  and mechatronics depth — large, precise, motorised, controlled products — is *how* DT delivers that,
  never the headline. Projection is the current output, not the identity.
- **Market reality (internal only).** Residential projection is shrinking: flat panels took the small
  and mid rooms, LED walls are taking the big ones. DT's projection base is now the very large room
  (~5 m screens) and boutique commercial. That base is the cash engine, not the future — the strategy
  is to redeploy the competence, as Future Automation did when it diversified off the display.
- **Flagship = the DT Screen Wall.** A modular *front-wall* solution for cinemas and screening rooms
  that mounts any screen and speaker configuration on a decoupled structural framework with a dense
  face, baffle-mounted speaker positions and acoustic control, installed rapidly (from DT's own ISE
  2023 description). It comes in projection *and* LED variants. It integrates DT + Pro-Fi + C-ATS in one
  structure, is display-agnostic by design, and is the clearest bridge across the projection→LED shift.
  Debuted ISE 2023, never productised (no page, no datasheet) — the biggest single gap to close.
- **Two other growth lines:** LED-wall engineering (mounting, alignment, service access, cooling,
  control) and motorised concealment & movement (lifts, drops, reveals — Future Automation's proven
  path).
- **Control stays embedded, and is never a line item. Decided.** The Commanders are the reason the
  machines work well, and they are sold only inside the products. The Dynamic Commander already ships
  that way — it isn't a purchasable SKU at all — and the **Actuator Commander is being withdrawn as a
  line item** so the rule holds without exception. Two reasons, both sound: selling controllers alone
  would make DT a components/OEM supplier, which it won't be; and a Commander you can buy is a Commander
  a competitor can buy and fit to their own mechanism, which would hand away the one advantage that
  can't otherwise be copied.
- **Products are driven by physical size, not per-model engineering.** What determines a mount,
  enclosure or port hole is the dimensions of the unit; the projector-model tags on the site are a
  sizing aid, not a claim of bespoke per-model fit.
- **Not fenced to home cinema** — boutique commercial too.
- **Name:** Display Technologies, DT for short. (DTScreens is a legacy social handle, not the name.)

## 2. Who it's for, and what their problem really is

**Positioning core**

> The buyer is the AV integrator, and the challenge in a high-end room is that the technology has to
> serve the experience without dominating the space. A projector, a large screen and a bank of speakers
> are big, demanding things — and the client wants a room that looks designed, not installed. That is
> what DT's products are for: to let all of it integrate into the architecture. Projectors are hidden
> and kept cool, images pass cleanly through the wall, screens and masking sit flush, and the whole
> front of the room becomes structure rather than equipment. The technology does its job and disappears
> into the design.

**The reasoning under it**

- **The concept is architectural integration** — the thread through the whole line. Mirror drops, port
  holes, hush boxes, vertical mounts and the Screen Wall each remove a piece of bulky or awkward AV from
  the finished space, so the room reads as designed rather than equipped.
- **These are products the integrator buys, not builds.** Never frame it as make-vs-buy; no integrator
  would fabricate these. The value is that DT engineers the integration so the integrator can deliver a
  clean architectural result.
- **This widens the audience to the architect and designer**, not just the integrator — because the
  room's *look* is at stake, integration is specified by whoever owns the design.
- **The Screen Wall is the fullest expression — and it's a performance element, not just aesthetic.**
  Integrating a large screen and the speakers behind it is fundamental to how the room performs:
  alignment, decoupling, speaker position and acoustic control all have to be right. With an
  acoustically-transparent projection screen that's demanding; with **LED — which is not acoustically
  transparent — integrating screen and speakers becomes a genuinely hard problem.** That is why the
  Screen Wall matters *more* as the market moves to LED, not less.
- **The stakes:** in a high-end room, visible or awkward AV wrecks the design the client is paying for.
  Integration isn't a nicety, it's the requirement.
- **Forward relevance:** architectural integration is display-agnostic — LED walls need integrating too
  — so this competence carries across the projection→LED shift.
- **Buyer-truth: an unasked question** ~~parked, as with the other brands~~ *(struck 2026-08-18, `Q58`/`PR-3` — the parking was ceremony; **and "as with the other brands" is how it spread**)* — the exact split (integrator vs specialist cinema
  contractor vs architect/designer) comes from real jobs, not invention.

## 3. The promise

**Positioning core**

> DT turns the big, complex physical parts of a room into products the integrator can simply specify and
> trust. The screen, the masking, the mounts and concealment, the front wall — each engineered, each
> sized to the equipment and the space, each arriving ready to integrate into the architecture and
> perform. What the integrator delivers is a room where the technology disappears into the design and
> does its job. And what they gain in DT is a maker whose competence follows the picture forward, from
> projector to LED — so the choice they make today still holds tomorrow.

**The reasoning under it**

- **The promise is integration and performance, delivered as a product.** Specify it, it arrives made,
  it integrates cleanly and it performs. Reliability is the heart of it — big, expensive elements that
  simply work.
- **The forward promise is the real differentiator: specifying DT is future-proof.** Because DT's
  competence is the integration of the large-format image — not projection — the integrator isn't
  betting on a fading technology.
- **The Screen Wall is where the promise is strongest** — the whole front of the room (screen, speakers,
  acoustics, structure) integrated and performing as one delivered product, across both projection and
  LED.
- **Control is embedded, which is why the machines just work** — it reinforces reliability rather than
  being a separate claim.
- **Honesty line.** The Screen Wall is real (ISE 2023) but under-productised, so "specify and trust" is
  partly aspirational until it has a proper spec and datasheet — a product-definition gap to close
  before the promise is fully deliverable.
- **Dealer-first:** the win is the integrator's clean, performing room and their future-proofed choice —
  never DT's engineering cleverness.

## 4. Why it's credible

**Positioning core**

> DT's credibility is in the hardware. It designs and builds these systems itself — engineered and
> manufactured in-house, not badged or bought in — and they're installed and working in some of the most
> prestigious home cinemas around. A masking screen that holds its geometry, a mount that carries a heavy
> projector without a tremor, a front wall that places screen and speakers exactly: this is engineering
> you can stand in front of and judge. The proof is the machine itself.

**The reasoning under it**

- **DT is a genuine manufacturer — that's the core of it.** It designs and builds in-house; the machines
  exist, work, and are installed. For heavy engineering, a working install is stronger proof than any
  datasheet.
- **Track record is real but under-documented** — the same gap as C-ATS and Fabric Walls. Prestigious
  installs, thin on documented case studies and photography. Content-phase job.
- **The engineering lead is real:** competitors have copied DT's designs (FA copied DT's CAD). Evidence
  of origin and leadership — but an **internal, attributed note only**, never public copy and never for
  legal use.
- **Demonstrability:** the Screen Wall was shown at ISE 2023; the hardware is judged by eye and hand —
  precision and reliability, like Fabric Walls' visible proof.
- **Honesty flags:**
  - Don't overclaim testing or certifications (the C-ATS lesson). DT's proof is the hardware and the
    installs, not a lab regime.
  - **Forward credibility is thinner.** The Screen Wall LED variant exists but isn't productised;
    LED-wall engineering and concealment/movement are directions, not proven product records yet. "We
    follow the picture to LED" is a fair *capability* claim, not yet a proven *track record*.

## 5. The discipline

Internal throughout — the rules that keep DT's positioning honest, un-fenced, and consistent with the
group.

**Identity**

- **DT makes solutions for the architectural integration of technology.** Not "big complex machines,"
  not "projection." The manufacturing and engineering depth is how it's delivered, never the headline.
- **Don't narrate projection's decline publicly** — that's internal. In public, DT is confident,
  display-agnostic engineering, not a business managing a downturn.

**Architectural integration is the concept**

- Lead with integration — the technology serving the experience without dominating the room.
- The Screen Wall is aesthetic integration *and* critical performance; LED makes that integration harder,
  which is DT's opportunity, not a threat.

**Products, control, sizing**

- These are products the integrator **buys**, never things they'd build. Never frame it as make-vs-buy,
  and never imply the dealer can't do something.
- **Control stays embedded — never a line item, no exceptions.** The Commanders sell only inside the
  products. Don't market a controller on its own, don't quote one, and don't let one be treated as a
  component: it would make DT an OEM supplier and hand a competitor the one advantage that can't be
  copied. **The distinction is purpose, not part:** a Commander may be supplied as a *service
  replacement* against an existing DT product — we have to be able to support our own installed base —
  but never as a component to design into someone else's mechanism.
- **Products are size-driven, not per-model bespoke.** Don't claim "engineered to your exact projector."

**No superlative arms race** *(group-wide)*

- Don't claim DT's element is *the* most complex or critical part of the project. State its complexity in
  its own terms. (Applies to every brand — C-ATS and Fabric Walls included.)

**Honesty**

- Proof is the hardware and real installs — not a lab regime or certifications DT doesn't hold. No
  invented figures.
- **Forward claims are capability, not track record.** Don't imply a mature LED product line that doesn't
  exist; the Screen Wall is real but under-productised — don't present it as finished and spec'd until it
  is.

**Competitors, partners, naming**

- **FA-copied-CAD is an internal, attributed note only** — never public, never for legal use. Position
  against rivals by capability, never disparagement.
- Don't name manufacturing suppliers; DT builds in-house — say that instead, it's a strength.
- **The name is Display Technologies; DT for short.**
- **The consumer tier has left DT — decided.** The former Enthusiast screens are now a Cinema Store
  exclusive DIY range and are no longer DT products
  (`../../group-strategy/commercial-model.md`). DT is trade-only, which is what its identity requires.
  DT provenance may be told as a story on that channel but never used as branding. Nothing in DT material
  should refer to an Enthusiast tier, and the premium **Dynamic** masking range must never be conflated
  with it.

**Group**

- **Acoustic design stays C-ATS's domain** — DT provides the Screen Wall's structure and decoupling;
  C-ATS designs the acoustic requirement. Align, don't duplicate (as with Fabric Walls).

## 6. How DT fits the group

**Positioning core**

> DT is where the technology meets the architecture — so in a room built by the group, it's the point
> where the picture, the sound and the structure come together. The screen integrates with the fabric
> surface, the speakers sit within the wall, the acoustic treatment works behind it, and the projector or
> LED disappears into the build. The Screen Wall makes this literal: one engineered front-of-room carrying
> the image, the speakers and the acoustics as a single structure. Get DT right and a room full of
> separate technologies resolves into one designed space that performs.

**The reasoning under it**

- **Architectural integration of technology is the connective concept across the group.** Fabric Walls is
  the aesthetic layer everything integrates into; DT integrates the *technology* into it; C-ATS the
  acoustics; Pro-Fi the sound. DT is the technology-integration node of the whole-room play.
- **The Screen Wall is the group-integration flagship** — DT structure + Pro-Fi speakers + C-ATS acoustics
  and decoupling, behind a Fabric Walls surface, in one product. The clearest embodiment of "one
  relationship, whole room."
- **Acoustic design stays C-ATS's domain — the alignment rule.** DT engineers the Screen Wall's structure
  and decoupling; C-ATS designs the acoustic requirement. Align, don't duplicate.
- **The concealment line ties to Fabric Walls** — mirror drops, hush boxes and port holes are set into or
  behind the fabric layer; acoustic-transparent screens put speakers and treatment behind the image.
- **Forward/strategic: DT is the group's route into LED integration.** As the display future shifts to
  LED, DT's integration competence — and the Screen Wall's LED variant — is what lets the group deliver
  coherent LED rooms. DT's pivot and group growth are the same move.
- **The handoff must feel like one group** — especially DT↔C-ATS (Screen Wall acoustics), DT↔Pro-Fi
  (speakers) and DT↔Fabric Walls (surface). Seamless, or the whole-room advantage leaks.

## 7. What's still open

Internal only — the unresolved items to settle before they harden into copy or product.

- **Turning the direction into sellable solutions.** The identity is set and the growth lines are named,
  but Screen Wall, LED-wall engineering and motorised concealment each still have to become concrete
  products with specs, pricing and a go-to-market. Clear direction; the translation is the work.
- **The Screen Wall product-definition gap.** Real and exhibited (ISE 2023) but with no product page, no
  datasheet, no story. The flagship barely exists commercially. Productising it is the single
  highest-value job.
- **LED-wall engineering — scope it.** What DT actually builds and sells for LED (mounting, alignment,
  service access, cooling, control), and how far it goes.
- **Motorised concealment & movement — decide the range.** How much of Future Automation's territory DT
  takes, and which pieces.
- ~~**The Enthusiast range go-to-market**~~ — **RESOLVED, and out of DT.** The range was a good product
  undersold because it sat inside a trade brand with no reason to address consumers. It is now a Cinema
  Store exclusive DIY range and no longer DT's problem or DT's product
  (`../../group-strategy/commercial-model.md`). Naming and range definition are decided there.
- **Forward proof.** LED and future-proof claims are capability, not track record. They need built LED
  installs and a productised Screen Wall to become provable.
- **Track-record documentation.** Prestigious installs exist but case studies and photography are thin —
  same gap as C-ATS and Fabric Walls.
- **Artmask.** A new art-masking category appears on the site but not in the product feed. Understand its
  role and how it sits against Screen Research's art masking (which we judged compromised and unpopular).
  Reconcile.
- **Buyer-truth — an unasked question, not a parked decision.** The real split: AV integrator vs specialist cinema
  contractor vs architect/designer. ~~From real jobs, not invented. Parked.~~ **Struck** (`../../registers/questions.md`
  `Q58`, `../../registers/premises.md` `PR-3`, 2026-08-18) — *the parking was ceremony, and on C-ATS the equivalent
  question was half-answered the first time it was put to an owner (`Q55`).* **And the actor cast is now confirmed at
  group level** (`../c-ats/claims.md` `C2.27`): client, architect, interior designer, AV consultant, integrator —
  *so the "split" question is narrower than it looked, and it is about **influence**, not identity.*
- **Pivot timing.** How fast projection declines and how aggressively to shift resource from the projection
  base to the growth lines. A sequencing decision.
