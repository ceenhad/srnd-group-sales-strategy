# The SRND Group sales & marketing strategy

*The top-level strategy in one document — what we are doing, why, where it goes, how it's created, what
tools are used, what we track. Drafted with Neil, 2026-08-17. Corrections land in this file, in place. The
detail beneath it lives in the rest of `group-strategy/` and `motion/`; this document governs them.*

## What we are doing

We are replacing the traditional sales rep with managed content, run as a system. Content does what a good
rep did — reach, recall, the technical answer, teaching, presence — and the small skilled team does what
only people can do: real conversations, judgement, and the impossible projects. The system runs on the
buyer journey: every piece of content exists to move a dealer through one specific gateway, every piece
points at its next step, and everything is signalled and tracked in engine.

## Why we are doing it

The rep model is arithmetically dead for us: a UK rep must generate £600–700k at ~30% gross margin to
justify existing, and twenty years of trying says it doesn't happen. Since covid the visit is unnecessary
anyway — but engagement is not optional, and the bar is exact: **add value in every discussion; a dealer
disengages the moment you know 1% less than they do.** We are strong precisely where that bar is set — we
know more — and weak at outbound, which neither owner wants to do and which doesn't scale regardless. We
handle inbound well. So the strategy is: content generates the engagement and the inbound; people handle
what comes in. Our engagement is currently at an all-time low while everything we know says regular, useful
cadence delivers — the failures were never the idea, they were ad hoc execution, scattered tools, and
untrackable data. This fixes those three things and nothing else.

## The journey it runs on

Six stages: a dealer who has never heard of us → knows the name → believes it could work → registers and
buys → the first job succeeds → orders again, across brands. Between stages sit gateways, each with an
observable signal:

1. **Hook lands** — a visit arriving from a hook (search, social, press, referral)
2. **Proposition lands** — depth engagement: reads, a tool used, a download, a sample request
3. **Registration** — the hinge; signed T&Cs, pricing access; signalled in engine
4. **First order** — in engine
5. **First job succeeds** — clean install, no escalation, a verification
6. **The loop** — a second order, and a second brand

Content is organised as **pathways**: chains of pieces that carry a dealer from one gateway to the next,
each piece with a defined job and a named destination. Hooks are many per product, varied by appeal — earn
more, save time, easier to do, better results, the problem named. Every touch adds value; a touch with no
value in it doesn't go out. Conversion is cumulative — many small useful touches, not one big campaign.

**Triggers make it a machine:** a tool submission gets its report with the next step attached; a
registration starts the welcome flow into the brand stream; a first order gets the install pack unprompted;
a live deal quiet past ~100 days gets the one-tap status ask. Signals fire these — not calendars, not
memory.

## Where it goes

Each brand owns its channels, and the cadence differs by channel (Neil, 2026-08-17, Q49):

- **Social carries the daily drumbeat, three platforms as one** — LinkedIn, Facebook and Instagram get the
  same content, scheduled, because a decent schedule tool makes three no more effort than one. Social
  carries the hooks, not the substance.
- **The brand site's knowledge base** is the canonical home of the answers, and **the brand YouTube
  channel** carries the video — both run weekly, not daily.
- **The brand mail stream** is the direct channel and follows **one group email policy** (drafted in
  `motion/content.md`, awaiting Neil's correction) — sent from engine once engine mail exists, from the
  existing tool with brand segmentation until then.

The group channel carries group material only. The store is where every path ultimately points. We feed the
channels with history in them rather than starting new ones.

## How it's created

The production line. An owner's answer — recorded once, on camera or a call, or mined from years of sent
mail that already answered these questions — becomes the derivative set: the article, the clip, the hook
variants, the mail item, the datasheet paragraph. Claude does the labour; a human approves in minutes;
nothing publishes without a truth-check. The product records and the databases are the feed, so an answer
is made once and reused forever. Content does not need to be sophisticated — it needs to be right, useful,
and regular. The judgement stays human; only the labour moved.

## The tools

**Engine is the hub**: accounts, pricing, orders, the inbound log, mail sending, the account × brand signal
matrix, link tags — replacing the scattered third-party tools over time, each replacement inheriting the
old tool's numbers as its acceptance test. Around it: the studio, the Experience Centre, the render
library, the design tools (wired to capture leads into engine, which they currently don't), and Claude as
the production line. Formal accounting and ambient analytics stay outside.

## What we track

- **The funnel, per gateway, per brand** — conversion counted at each stage like a shop. The stalled
  gateway is next month's content brief; that feedback loop is what keeps this alive in month six.
- **The drumbeats** — did each brand communicate today; how many real conversations this week.
- **Inbound per period** — the priority-one number, counted in engine.
- **Accounts created → first orders** — the conversion that is currently the whole sales problem.
- **Active dealers per year** — the asset accumulating, or not.

Benchmarks are always our own last numbers, never industry figures. Every published link carries a tag.
Numbers land in engine and arrive without anyone compiling them.

## The operating rhythm

Something from every brand, through its channels, every day. Real conversations as often as possible. A
weekly look at the funnel numbers. A monthly note per brand carrying each segment's next pathway step. The
queue fills itself from three sources: the worst-converting gateway, the questions arriving inbound, and
the signals firing.

## Then the brands

With this locked, each brand gets the same treatment from its own material: its pathways built stage by
stage, its hooks written from its products, its segments cut from the database — the contact list, the
product records, fifteen years of history — and its channels switched on at the drumbeat. Pro-Fi and
Light Walls launch *into* this system rather than beside it.
