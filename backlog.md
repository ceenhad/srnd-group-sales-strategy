# Backlog — the question first, then the register

*This file sorts the repo's actions — but sorted under the question they exist to answer, not under a
calendar. The high-level question the whole strategy turns on is whether **content can do the sales rep's job
for this group**. `group/08-sales-motion.md` argues it to its limit and `decided.md` S1–S9 commit to it as
direction — but a direction chosen is not a question answered. It is answered only in operation: a published
answer retiring a question, a dealer arriving already familiar, a dealer we have never met converted by the
corpus alone. Until those signals exist, implementation schedules are premature. So there are no weeks here
and no milestones — the structure is: the question in its parts, the two tests that answer it, what is
deliberately *not* a test of it, and then the full register as the reference layer beneath.*

---

## The question, in its parts

The rep's job was broken down in `group/08-sales-motion.md`: content takes reach, recall, the technical
answer, teaching and the specifier route; it cannot qualify, discover, close, want the order or bring back
market intelligence. That split turns "content as the sales rep" into five sub-questions, each with a
different state and a different way of being answered.

### Q1 — Can a published answer actually retire a question?

The atom of the whole model (`decided.md` S14), settled in principle and never yet observed. The nearest
evidence — the ~9,500-view reverberation explainer — proves *reach*, not *retirement*; nothing published has
yet been watched to see whether the question stopped arriving.

**Answered by:** publishing the two written one-pagers and the three recordings (DOC-1, DOC-2) where dealers
actually look, then watching the two known site-failure questions. The signal needs no instrumentation — an
owner notices they have stopped answering it.

### Q2 — Can this team sustain the cadence at all?

**The genuinely open half of the thesis, and it is two decisions, not a project.** S20 settles that nothing
comes off anyone's plate; what is not set is the floor rate (CON-1) and confirmed publication ownership
(CON-2). Without both there is no rep, only intentions — the repo's own stall signal (a month with no
publication) is the check, and it needs no schedule to apply.

### Q3 — Does a face build the familiarity a rep's repeat visits used to?

No precedent exists: eight years and ~54,500 views across the estate and not one piece has a presenter
(`group/04-content.md`). This is a format with no history here, so it is a test, not a rollout (CON-4, one
batch session). **The signal:** a first conversation that opens with *"I've watched your videos."*

### Q4 — Can the corpus convert dealers we have never met?

The market test, and the strategy has already named its instrument: **the US C-ATS entry is explicitly a
controlled test of "content is the rep"** (`group/01-commercial-model.md`) — no legacy relationships, no body
on the ground, no show this year; only the corpus, search, and personal authority. Either answer is valuable,
and a negative is best learned in a market we are not currently earning from. (US-5.)

### Q5 — Do the substitutes for what content *cannot* do actually exist?

The thesis fails quietly here whatever the content achieves, because these are the parts of the rep's job
nobody has picked up:

- **Qualification** → the tools. Exists; closing its own loop is what's left (TOOL-1).
- **Discovery** → the spec conversation. We are in it on most jobs and capture nothing (XS-2 — not started).
- **Wanting the order** → a person. Currently unowned (XS-3).
- **Market intelligence** → no substitute exists. A recorded hole, held rather than argued away.

### Held, not answered now — the risks inside the thesis

Named in `open-items.md` and worth keeping visible rather than solving: this is a *thinner* insurance policy
than a sales team (if the content isn't made there is no fallback motion); the model still rests on two or
three people; and putting faces on the moat concentrates it further.

---

## How the question gets answered — two tests, both already in the plan

1. **The retirement test — at home, nearly free, starts from what is already written.** Publish DOC-1/DOC-2,
   watch whether the questions stop arriving (Q1). Simultaneously forces Q2's two decisions, because nothing
   publishes without a rate and an owner.
2. **The market test — US C-ATS (Q4).** The corpus against a market where nothing else is working for us.

**And the jigsaw point — why this is group-level and where the brands fit.** The group holds the question
once; the brands are where it gets tested, and they are not interchangeable: **C-ATS is the test brand** for
Q1 and Q4 because it alone has the documentation standard and the demonstrated search pull; **DT is where
Q3's audience already is** (three-quarters of the video estate). The other brands *inherit the answer* rather
than re-running the test — which is what stops six brands doing this six times.

## What is deliberately *not* a test of the thesis

These proceed on their own merits and prove nothing about content — keeping them separate is what stops
their progress being mistaken for the question being answered:

- **US DT recovery** — conversion of a warm base, personal and terms-led, explicitly not dependent on the
  content programme (`decided.md` C1j, C1k).
- **Store and site hygiene** (W5) — removing live contradictions with settled decisions.
- **Network pruning** (W3) — removing barriers.
- **Partner acts** (W7) — relationship, done as acts.
- **The Screen Wall page** (W10) — a build the whole-room story needs regardless.

# The register

*The reference layer: every discrete action in the repo, extracted. Each workstream is tagged by its relation
to the question — **answers it**, **prerequisite** (the corpus is what the rep speaks from), or
**independent** (proceeds on its own merits). No dates, no weeks.*

**How to read it.** Not everything is a project, and the Type column is doing real work: **Do** = work that
takes time; **Decide** = a choice only a named person can make, usually minutes; **Fetch** = a fact to look
up, no judgement required. **Status:** `ready` · `in progress` · `blocked` (named blocker) · `done` ·
`parked`. **Owners are named only where the repo names them** — `—` means unowned, which is information
rather than a gap to guess at. **When an item completes:** mark it done here, land evidence in `decided.md`,
don't pass back through `group/`. **IDs are stable and rows regular** so the register can be lifted into
engine or any tracker when the process gets a system home.
