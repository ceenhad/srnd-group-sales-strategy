# The direction of travel — the group level, from the data

*`GRP-1`. Written 2026-08-16 at Neil's direction: **"we are trying to do a group strategy here… I think you have
the data to understand the direction of travel at the group level. Then we create question series we sweep
through for each brand."** This is that statement, and it precedes all brand-level work.*

**Why this file exists, in a sentence:** the group's own position had never been stated from the measured
record, and every brand sweep now depends on it.

**Method.** Read from `data/derived/all-transactions.csv` — 10,360 lines, five entities, fifteen years,
external counterparties only. **This is a fresh read, not a summary of `archive-findings.md`**, and where it
differs from an earlier finding the difference is stated. *Caveat: totals here come to £19.81m against finding
14's £19,443,934; the ~2 % gap is a difference in intra-group exclusion, unreconciled and not material to
anything below.*

## 1 · The business as it now measures

**Forget fifteen years. The distribution business ended in 2023, so the business that exists is 2024 onward.**

| | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 *(to mid-Aug)* |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **Total** | £1,342k | £1,447k | £1,096k |

**£3,884,955 across the period, on a rising line** — 2026 annualises near £1.7m. **The group is growing, and
the rebuild after distribution wound down has worked.** That is the first thing to say plainly, because none of
the documents in this repo say it.

### And it is a manufacturer now, by margin if not yet by habit

| 2024–26 | Revenue | Share | **Margin** | **Share of margin** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Own-made** | £2,250,403 | 58.2 % | **£1,179,211** | **69.2 %** |
| **Carried** | £1,365,683 | 35.3 % | £273,137 | 16.0 % |
| **Services** | £251,273 | 6.5 % | **£251,273** | **14.7 %** |
| | **£3,867,359** | | **£1,703,621** | **44.1 % blended** |

*(Ladder rates from finding 23: carried 20 %, own-made direct 52.4 %, services ≈100 %.)*

**Three things fall straight out.**

- **Own-brand share is 58 %, not 34 %.** Finding 15's 34 % measured *SRND Group the entity*; at group level, with
  DT included, the shift the 2016 make-not-buy decision aimed at **has already happened.**
- **Services earn almost as much margin as the entire carried book** — 14.7 % against 16.0 % — **on less than a
  fifth of the revenue.**
- **Every £1 of revenue carries 44p of margin**, and the number moves entirely with mix.

### But 2026 went backwards, and one account did it

| 2026 to mid-Aug | Share |
|---|---:|
| Carried | **50.4 %** |
| Own-made | 46.2 % |
| Services | 3.5 % |

**The 2026 mix is worse than 2024's, and the cause is a single Leyard Komodo order** — £472,320 of LED wall to
one account. *Not a failure: it is £94k of margin and the strategy holds carried lines deliberately. But it
shows the mix is one order wide.*

## 2 · What the group actually is, measured

### It is DT, with three smaller lines attached

| Own-made, 2024–26 | | 2026 only |
|---|---:|---:|
| **DT** | **73.3 %** | **79.6 %** |
| Fabric Walls | 14.7 % | 14.1 % |
| C-ATS | 8.4 % | 4.2 % |
| Light Walls | 3.6 % | 2.1 % |

**"Six brands, one group" describes the intent and the catalogue. It does not describe the revenue.**
Own-made is DT and a Fabric Walls business, with C-ATS and Light Walls at the margin — **and DT's share is
rising, not falling.** *Pro-Fi is pre-revenue; SRND Distribution is the carried book.*

### It is acquiring dealers faster than at any time since the distribution era

| | new counterparties | active that year |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2019–23 average | 29 | 76 |
| **2024** | **73** | 95 |
| **2025** | **56** | 94 |
| 2026 *(part)* | 25 | 60 |

**154 new counterparties in under three years.** *This is the single most encouraging number in the dataset and
it appears nowhere in the plan.* **The acquisition engine is not broken.**

### And they arrive small, buy one thing, and mostly stay that way

**181 accounts with revenue in 2024–26. Median £4,900. Ninety-three of them under £5,000. Ninety-nine of 182
bought exactly one category.**

**Breadth is where the money is, and the archive's finding replicates in the new business:**

> **The 27 % of accounts that buy three or more categories are 79 % of revenue.**
> *(The distribution era's version: 44 % multi-line dealers were 87.7 % of revenue — finding 2.)*

**And the shape of the good accounts is specific:** the **31 accounts that buy both own-made and carried are
64.6 % of all revenue**, against 121 own-made-only accounts making up 29 %.

### It is concentrated, and increasingly on one name

**Top account 15.8 % · top five 41.1 % · top ten 54.5 %.**

**Hifi Corner is £1,167,034 lifetime and £539,054 in 2026 alone** — its best year ever, and the largest account
in the business. **Its purchase is almost entirely carried: LED walls £472,320, speakers and amplifiers
£154,947, projectors £99,910, processors £72,344 — plus £143,901 of services.**

> **The group's biggest account buys carried product and services, and almost no own-made.** That is the attach
> problem in one line, and it is not hypothetical: **£472,320 of Komodo against £38,452 of own-made Screen Wall
> across the entire group.**

### And the service business is one customer

**£251,273 of services in 2024–26, from 28 accounts — but the named, modern services are essentially one
name.** Hifi Corner bought the **£3,000 Acoustic Treatment Design Service**, the **£7,500 Sound Isolation
Design Project** and the **£6,480 Fabric Walls Madrid design**. *The older seam — 22 C-ATS design engagements at
a median £1,560 — went largely to Apex-Tech, and Apex has gone.*

**This qualifies `XS-5` sharply: the price precedents are real but thin, and they are not evidence of demand
across the base.** *What they are evidence of is that **when an account is offered the whole thing, it buys the
whole thing.***

## 3 · The direction of travel, stated

> ## ▲ Priority one, set by Neil in the group session, 2026-08-16
>
> ***"We know people but engagement is terrible. Increasing and maintaining engagement is priority one. We can
> only sell to engaged partners."***
>
> **This sits above everything below, and the section under it was written a level too low.** The analysis
> that follows — breadth, the second category, the missing artefact — describes **what selling to an engaged
> base would look like.** *It presumes engagement. That presumption is what is wrong.*
>
> **And the measured record supports him bluntly.** The relationships exist and the contact does not:
>
> | | |
> |---|---|
> | **603 counterparties** ever; **~95 active** in any recent year | **16 %** |
> | **348 hold an account**; **189 have never bought** | finding 27 |
> | **29 email campaigns** produced **6 clicks** to `c-ats.co.uk` | finding 29 |
> | **The best campaign ever sent was the last one sent** — four months ago | `MON-16` |
> | Monday's per-brand subscribe flags **never reached the sending system** | finding 28 |
> | **40 approved dealers cannot see a price** | `MON-13` |
> | **Inbound is uncountable** — it lands in mailboxes, calls and heads | `14-engine-as-hub.md` §1 |
>
> **So "engagement is terrible" is not a mood, it is the one thing every measurement in this repo agrees on** —
> and *"we can only sell to engaged partners"* explains why the 99 single-category accounts stay single-category.
> **Nobody has spoken to them.**
>
> **What changes below:** breadth is not the priority — **it is the symptom, and the expected result of fixing
> the priority.** The machinery still matters, but it is what you use *once a partner is engaged*, not what
> creates engagement.

> ### ▲ And the repo already names the answer — found on a proper read, 2026-08-16
>
> **`05-channels.md` has been carrying it, and this document was written without it:**
>
> > *"A search turns up **no interviews, no bylined technical writing and no speaking presence** under his own
> > name, and nothing at all on the design or architecture side… **personal authority is not a side channel
> > serving specifiers — it is the primary sales instrument**, serving dealers and specifiers with the same
> > work. That is the strongest argument for building it, and the reason its absence is **the most expensive
> > gap on this page**."*
>
> **So "engagement is terrible" and "the most expensive gap is dormant personal authority" are the same
> finding, arrived at from two directions.** *Neil is credited on Cinema Tools as a well-known cinema
> designer, the moat is explicitly person-concentrated (`00-strategy.md`), the EI feature was built on two
> decades in the industry — and none of it is anywhere a dealer or a specifier would meet it.*
>
> **It is also the cheapest answer available**, because it plays to something that already exists rather than
> building a programme: it is the face the content model needs (`04-content.md`, component 3), the route to
> pre-tender specification (`02-buyer-journey.md`), and the thing that makes a dealer feel they know us before
> they meet us. **One asset, three gaps.**

**The group is a manufacturer that still sells like a distributor.**

Manufacturing is 58 % of revenue and 69 % of margin. Acquisition is at a fifteen-year high. But **what happens
after acquisition is a distributor's motion**: a dealer asks for a thing, a store quote goes out listing that
thing, and 99 of 182 accounts never buy a second category. **The catalogue is a group; the selling is
line-by-line.**

**Three structural gaps, in order of what they cost.**

| | Gap | Measured cost | Why it persists |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Breadth per account** | 79 % of revenue comes from 27 % of accounts. **99 accounts buy one category** | **There is no artefact that can carry an unrequested layer.** A store quote lists what was asked for (`Q9`) — *but see the qualification below; some of those 99 are not a failure* |
| **2** | **Services** | Highest margin in the group, **28 accounts, and the named ones are one account** | **There is no offer.** Nothing is defined or costed, so it cannot be sold *or* given away meaningfully (`Q34`) |
| **3** | **Concentration** | One account is 15.8 %; **2026's whole mix moved on one order** | Nothing structural — but it is the reason breadth is a risk question, not only a growth one |

**All three are the same shape: the group is good at getting dealers and poor at growing them.** *That is a
better diagnosis than "we are undersold", because it says where.*

> **One qualification the buyer journey already makes, and this document should not have needed reminding
> of.** `02-buyer-journey.md`: ***"A single-brand dealer is not a failed dealer. Some accounts will only ever
> be one brand, and that's a good outcome. If the trail treats them as incomplete it will push adjacency at
> people who don't want it — the oversaturation risk."***
>
> **So "99 accounts buy one category" is a number, not a verdict.** *Some of those 99 are fabric contractors,
> or acoustic specialists, or LED buyers, and always will be. What the number cannot distinguish is a dealer
> who **won't** buy a second layer from one who **hasn't been asked** — and only the second is a problem.*
> **That distinction is not in the data and cannot be got from it**; it comes from the engagement work, which
> is another reason engagement precedes breadth rather than following it.

## 4 · The growth arithmetic, three ways, all measured

**The target is 10–20 % on ~£1.45m: £145–290k.** It is available three separate times over, and no route needs
a new market.

1. **Breadth.** Move **twenty single-category accounts to three categories.** At the current median that is
   ~£100k of revenue but at *good-account* rates far more — the 3+ cohort averages **£65k** against the
   single-category median of £4,900.
2. **Services.** They ran at £251k across 28 accounts with no offer, no price list and no promotion. **Doubling
   the number of accounts that buy one is ~£250k of near-pure margin** — the equivalent of **£1.25m of carried
   revenue.**
3. **The signed base.** 348 hold accounts, **189 have never bought** (finding 27). **Ten to sixty-four first
   orders.**

> **The three compound rather than compete**, and each is served by the same machinery: a filled record, a block
> library, an artefact that can carry a layer nobody asked for, and a service offer with prices on it.

## 5 · What this changes in the plan

- **Stop describing the group as six brands when the revenue is DT plus Fabric Walls.** *The brand sweep should
  be weighted by that, not run evenly across six.*
- **The record's job is now clearly commercial, not documentary.** It feeds the block library, `N10` prices the
  design work, and both exist to make an account's second and third category possible.
- **▲ Engagement is the headline, and breadth is how you read whether it worked.** *Revised in session,
  2026-08-16.* **Categories per account** stays the lagging measure — computable today, tracked nowhere — but
  the leading one is **engagement, which has no definition and no instrument yet.** *That is the next thing to
  settle, and it is not a metric problem: `14-engine-as-hub.md` §1 already reset "engaged" to **active inbound
  communication** and then found inbound uncountable.*
- **Acquisition needs no work.** *Nothing in the plan should spend effort on getting more dealers* — 154
  arrived in three years and **99 of them bought once, because nothing happened after they arrived.**
- **The carried book is doing its job and should be left alone.** It funds the own lines and it brought in the
  account that buys the most services.

## 6 · What this does not answer

- **Why** accounts stop at one category — the data shows the pattern, never the reason. *No loss reason exists
  in any system (finding 19), and the spec conversation is still uncaptured.*
- **Whether the 3+ accounts are broad because they are big, or big because they are broad.** *The archive says
  first-order size predicts nothing, which points at the second, but that is inference.*
- **What a complete SRND room costs.** The nearest measurement is **CUK's £78,445 across five categories in one
  year, 2024** — which the repo records as a demo room. *If that is what it was, it is the only priced example
  of the whole-room proposition in fifteen years of data.*
- **Anything about Pro-Fi**, which has no revenue to read.
