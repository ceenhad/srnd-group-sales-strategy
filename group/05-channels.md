# Channels

*The real, named landscape to choose from. Channel **types** are in `02-buyer-journey.md`; placement is in
`04-content.md`.*

> **A candidate landscape, not a media plan.** Three disciplines: **select, don't adopt wholesale** (which
> channels, at what priority and spend, is a decision — `../open-items.md`); **verify before committing**,
> because trade media and events merge, rebrand and close; and **online-first — print is dead, consumer print
> especially.** Judge every channel by its online value: the site, newsletter, podcast and any microsite we
> hold there is the asset; the magazine is incidental.

## Social

**LinkedIn** (business and personal) is the primary B2B channel — the trade lives there. **Instagram** for

## Communities

- **AVForums** (UK) and **AVS Forum** (US) — large home-cinema communities, mostly enthusiast with some trade.
- **AVForums is a live asset, not a candidate.** We built their editorial cinema ("Picture House 4") in trade
  and hold roughly **£10,000 in owed video and editorial content** — unusable until now for want of a
  consumer-facing destination. The Cinema Store DIY range is that destination, and the audience match is close
  to exact.
  - **The existing content is history, not an asset.** They published an 18-video "Home Cinema Build Diary" on
    that room, but the build was **around ten years ago**, well before SRND existed, so it carries no group
    branding. That's chronology, not neglect — the kit, techniques and room are long superseded. Treat it as
    evidence of a long-standing relationship, useful when negotiating, and nothing more.
  - **So spend the credit on new content.** Video first (the group has almost none); brief it rather than
    script it, because editorial independence is the value; and point it at **Cinema Store, never the trade
    brands.** That separation is what keeps it safe.
- **CEDIA member community** and LinkedIn groups — trade-to-trade, where peer referral lives.

## Two gaps: non-cinema, and specifiers

This list is overwhelmingly residential-CI and cinema. Two absences matter.

**Non-cinema channels.** Commercial fit-out, hospitality and workplace reach different people through
different places, largely absent here. A real gap in the beyond-cinema determination (`00-strategy.md`) — and
one to fill once there's proof to carry into them, which those markets are more likely to permit us to show.

**Specifier channels — the bigger gap.** Architects, AV consultants and interior designers write pre-tender
specifications (`02-buyer-journey.md`), and almost nothing here reaches them. That machinery is different in
kind from trade press: **RIBA-approved CPD seminars**, **NBS specification clauses**, **BIM/Revit object
libraries**, specifier-grade technical downloads, finish and sample libraries, and the design press and bodies
— Dezeen and the luxury-interiors titles, **BIID / SBID**, **RIBA**, **AVIXA** for the consultant side, and
the Architects' Journal tier. The two gaps are really one route: those specifiers work across every room type,
so reaching them is also how we reach the adjacent markets.

## Worked evaluation: Essential Install

The pattern to follow for the others.

- **The SRND microsite — `essentialinstall.com/srnd/` — which we already pay for.** A brand-controlled hub on
  EI's high-authority trade domain, aggregating company pages we author plus SRND-tagged news, carried by
  their audience, search authority and newsletter. Effectively a **rented landing surface with a built-in
  trade audience** — part owned, part earned.
- **Current state: under-fed.** A handful of company pages, some posted July 2026, with thin prior activity.
  Paid for and running near-empty.
- **What good looks like:** feed it regularly with company pages and **virtual case studies**, each linking
  back to the relevant brand site; get them pushed through EI's news, newsletter and podcast; treat it as
  top-of-funnel plus proof placement that borrows EI's audience and SEO.
- **Also available to earn into:** their News, Case Studies (Home Cinema / Whole Home / Hospitality /
  Commercial — a natural home for virtual case studies), Features, Guides, Interviews, the podcast and the
  newsletter.

  21 January 2026 (Daniel J Sait), in their Who's Who / Immersive Audio & Video section — which reads as

  this repo assumed**: it already states the beyond-cinema position ("private residential, commercial,
  cultural, wellness and experiential sectors"), already names **designers and consultants** alongside
  integrators, and already advertises the room-kit ladder ("from single-product specifications to fully
  modular, pre-engineered room solutions"). See the fix list in `../open-items.md` for where it contradicts
  what we have since settled.

**Action:** activate and feed it. Already paid for, already empty — the cheapest thing on this page.

## Personal authority — the underused channel, and the specifier route

Neil is credited on Cinema Tools as a "well-known cinema designer," the moat is explicitly
person-concentrated (`00-strategy.md`), and the EI feature is built on his two decades in the industry. Yet a
search turns up no interviews, no bylined technical writing and no speaking presence under his own name, and
nothing at all on the design or architecture side.

That matters more than it looks, because **the route into pre-tender specification runs through personal
expert authority rather than brand marketing.** A CPD seminar is delivered by a person; specifiers take advice
from experts, not vendors. So the dormant personal authority and the specifier gap
(`02-buyer-journey.md`) are the same opportunity — and it is far cheaper than building a marketing
programme, because it plays to a strength that already exists.

*Context worth holding:* Neil's own background is **distribution** — a high-end AV and home-cinema
distribution business preceded the manufacturing. So questions about competing with or selling through the
trade distributors (`06-competitors.md`) meet experience rather than speculation.
