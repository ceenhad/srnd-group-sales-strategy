# Execution standards

Good execution falls straight out of the buyer journey (`03-buyer-journey.md`), and it is largely a
matter of process. It is mostly a checklist worked through without gaps and timed right — the skill
is completeness and timing, not brilliance. That's good news: it's enumerable.

- **No gaps.** Every stage has an asset and a touchpoint — nowhere the dealer stalls with nothing to
  move them on.
- **Every stage hands to the next.** A clear next step, end to end. This is the core of it — the
  journey only continues if each stage points at the one after it. No dead ends.
- **Right thing, right moment.** Triggered by the dealer's state changing (visited, ordered, went
  quiet), not by our sending schedule.
- **Friction removed at the point of purchase** (stage 4) — the barriers resolved exactly where they
  bite.
- **A real process for the next order** (stage 6) — not hoping they come back.
- **Clean cross-brand handoff** — the group must feel like one group at every stage-6 cross-sell, or
  the compounding leaks (`01-dealer-as-asset.md`).

This standard applies at two scales. Each *piece* of content must hook, carry, prove, and hand off
on its own. The whole *journey* must do the same: cover every stage, wire every handoff, time every
trigger. Same discipline, one level up.

> **Group vs brand.** The *checklist* is group-owned — it's the shared definition of good execution,
> and the cross-brand handoff is only meaningful at group level. Filling it — the actual assets at
> each stage — is brand execution, done in each `brands/<brand>/`.
