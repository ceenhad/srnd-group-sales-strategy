# The production engine — studio + Experience Centre

Two owned, **group-shared** assets make "well" affordable and turn the direct-global model from
aspiration into something a small team can actually run. They serve every brand; their output is
brand-specific, their existence is not.

- **The studio** (in-house, for podcast/video) is the engine that makes rep-quality content cheap to
  produce at volume — the only way to cover every stage of the wheel *and* stay always-on (the
  persistent presence stage 6 depends on). It's also what makes attacking a new channel affordable:
  a new channel needs re-pointed content, not a new machine. This is why a **content plan is
  essential** per brand: the studio is what lets one small team rep every precious dealer,
  everywhere, continuously. Output maps straight onto the wheel — YouTube explainers (stages 1–2),
  how-to/install video (stages 3, 5), recorded demos (stage 3), podcast (stage 6 presence, and peer
  reach → referral).
- **The Experience Centre** (Kirkbymoorside) is the highest-trust touchpoint, but it's physical and
  scarce — we can't fly a global dealer base to North Yorkshire. So the studio's job is to
  **virtualise it**: film the demos, capture "hear/see the difference," turn the room into content a
  dealer anywhere can experience remotely. **Studio × Experience Centre = a demo you can distribute
  globally** — precisely what a direct, no-local-rep model would otherwise lack. This pairing may be
  the single most valuable owned asset in the group.

> **Why this is group-owned.** One studio and one Experience Centre serve all brands. Duplicating
> them per brand would defeat the economics that make the whole direct-global model runnable. Brands
> book capacity and define output; they don't own the assets.
