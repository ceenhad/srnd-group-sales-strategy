# Beyond cinema

*An explicit strategic determination, and the fences that have to come down for it. Internal.*

## The determination

High-end cinema got us here. It built the reputation, the engineering, the reference rooms and most of
the revenue. It is not where the group intends to stay.

**In trade, we are determined to escape being just a high-end cinema supplier.** Stated plainly because it has
been implicit for too long, and because half the corrections in this repo turn out to be the same
instruction applied brand by brand: state the problem, not the room; don't fence to cinema; "Walls" is
only the name; DT is not the projection company; Pro-Fi serves hospitality and wellness too. Those were
not five separate edits. They were one determination, showing up five times.

This is not a pivot away from cinema. Cinema remains the heartland of revenue and credibility, and
throwing that away would be foolish. It is the removal of a **self-imposed fence.**

## Scope: trade only. B2C stays cinema.

**This is a trade strategy and applies to trade only.** In B2C the opposite discipline holds: the consumer
proposition stays firmly **DIY home cinema**, and broadening it would be a recipe for disaster.

The reasons are specific rather than squeamish. The general consumer market is vast, fiercely competitive
and low-margin; we hold no authority outside cinema with consumers; and the communities we can actually
reach — AVForums and its like — *are* cinema communities. Everything that makes the consumer channel viable
comes from being the people who know cinema rooms.

So `cinema-store.com` is a DIY home cinema destination and its name is correct rather than limiting. (An
earlier draft of this document flagged the name as awkward on the assumption the consumer audience might
broaden; that assumption is wrong, and the flag is withdrawn.) See
`19-direct-and-carried-lines.md`.

## Why it's credible: the competences were never cinema-specific

Nothing the group is actually good at belongs to cinema. Only the history does.

- **Acoustics** — reflection, resonance and reverberation belong to rooms, not to their purpose. Every
  small room has them; large rooms are reverberation-dominated. Isolation is a building problem.
- **Surfaces** — a stretch-fabric system that conceals services and finishes a space is wanted anywhere
  an interior has to look considered and hide what's behind it.
- **Architectural integration of technology** — making equipment serve a space without dominating it is
  the universal problem of putting technology into designed rooms.
- **Audio** — reproduction is one job; **masking and ambience are another entirely**, and they belong to
  venues rather than cinemas.
- **Lighting** — addressable colour-critical lighting is an interiors capability.
- **Precision manufacture and embedded control** — large, moving, controlled assemblies. Nothing about
  that is cinema.

Which is why the escape is available at all: we are not asking the competences to stretch, only the
market definition to stop being narrower than they are.

## Where the bigger world is

These are the adjacencies the brand work already surfaced. Recording them is not the same as choosing
them — prioritisation and sequencing remain a decision (`open-items.md`).

- **Commercial fit-out** — fabric interiors and acoustic control in offices, workplaces and public
  spaces. Already a named Fabric Walls service, and a market where the fabric is the *absorber* rather
  than acoustically transparent.
- **Hospitality and wellness** — the market for shaping an environment rather than reproducing a
  soundtrack: masking to a quiet floor, ambience as a designed condition, with real clinical evidence
  behind soundscape effects. Pro-Fi's Spatial origin.
- **Commercial AV, meeting and conference rooms** — already visible in DT's screen tags and Fabric
  Walls' office kits.
- **Boutique commercial cinema and DCI** — larger rooms and jobs; still cinema, but a different
  commercial dynamic and a defined C-ATS commercial objective model behind it.
- **Two-channel and critical listening rooms** — the same three problems, a different listener.
- **Luxury residential beyond the cinema room** — media rooms, living spaces, whole-house interiors.
- **LED-driven spaces** — retail, corporate and experiential environments where a large image needs
  integrating and its audio solving. Leyard territory, and where DT's competence carries.
- **Marine** — real but narrow; a compliance edge case, not a segment (`brands/c-ats/`).

## The fences to take down

**Copy habits.** The per-brand guardrails already say it: name the problem, not the room. Those rules
exist and simply have to be honoured (`brands/*/positioning.md` §5 in each case).

**The naming estate, which is a genuine constraint and not a nitpick.** Much of what we own is
cinema-named: `cinema-tools.com`, `cinema-store.com`, `cinema-expert.com`, and C-ATS's own legal name
(Cinema Acoustic Treatment Systems). That naming was earned and carries authority, but it does fence
perception, and a determination to escape cinema-only has to be honest about it. Not all of it is a
problem —

- **Cinema Tools** is a design-authority brand where the name is an asset, and its engines already span
  residential, commercial and studio work. Probably keep.
- **Cinema Store** is correct as it stands, because B2C deliberately stays cinema (above). No change
  needed; the open question there is only the *range's* name, not the store's.
- **C-ATS's expansion** is already flagged unresolved — and this is another reason not to hard-code
  "Cinema" into it.

**The proof estate.** Nearly all our reference material is cinema. Non-cinema work needs its own proof,
or the escape stays theoretical: the first commercial fit-out or hospitality room we can show matters
more than any amount of repositioning.

**Channel habit.** The channels documented in `12-channels.md` are overwhelmingly residential-CI and
cinema. Commercial fit-out, hospitality and workplace reach different people through different places —
a real gap in the channel work, and a different buyer to learn.

## What stays

Cinema is the proving ground and should keep leading where it is genuinely strongest. The discipline is
narrow and specific: **lead with cinema credibility, never with a cinema-only definition.** Being known
as the people who solve the hardest rooms is an asset in any market; being known as a cinema supplier is
the fence.
