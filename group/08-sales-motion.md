# The sales motion

*How a sale gets made with no salesforce. Its own document because the sales half of sales and marketing had
outgrown a section of the commercial model — the reasoning for having no field force stays in
`01-commercial-model.md`, the craft of making the content is in `04-content.md`, and this is the motion itself.*

## The premise, and the two properties that make it more than a workaround

Content does the rep's job (`01-commercial-model.md`). That began as a necessity — sales people have not worked
here across many years — but it is worth holding for two reasons that a field force can never match:

- **It scales globally at close to zero marginal cost.** A rep covers a territory. A published answer covers
  every territory at once, in every time zone, permanently.
- **The time invested compounds.** A rep's best explanation is consumed in the room it was given in. A
  published answer keeps working, and keeps being found, long after the hour that made it.

Both are true in principle and neither is automatic. Most of this document is about the conditions that make
them actually hold, because our own estate is proof that they don't hold by default: 54 videos on the group
YouTube channel have produced 1,471 lifetime views (`04-content.md`).

## What content takes outright — and wins on

- **Being found.** A rep knocks on doors; content is what gets knocked on. Search and AI answers reach dealers
  no patrol could visit, in territories we will never have a body in.
- **Presence and recall.** Always-on beats occasional visits for being remembered when the dealer's next
  project finally lands — which happens on their client's clock, not ours.
- **The technical answer, and this is the important one.** Our content comes from the people who decided what
  the product does. A multi-line distributor's rep carries thirty brands and cannot be deep in any of them; a
  manufacturer's own rep would be relaying what the factory told them. So the strongest form of the idea isn't
  that content substitutes for a rep — it is that **content is a better rep than anyone could hire, at the part
  of the job that decides the sale.** Owning the factory is what makes that true
  (`03-partner-programme.md`).
- **Teaching, which is the moat delivered.** Training and certification are content, and they make the dealer
  better at their job — the thing we claim to be for.
- **Reaching specifiers at all.** You cannot hire reps to call on architects. CPD seminars, NBS clauses, BIM
  objects and specifier-grade downloads are all content, so pre-tender specification (`02-buyer-journey.md`) is
  only reachable under this model — a route a field force would never have opened.

## What content cannot do, and what substitutes instead

The real risk isn't overestimating content's reach; it is assuming it covers these and leaving them unowned.

- **Qualification** — is this dealer real, is this project real. **The substitute is the tools.** A design tool
  reveals a live project and its requirements, which is exactly what a rep spent a day in a car to learn. That
  is why lead capture out of Cinema Tools matters more than its size suggests (`07-tools.md`).
- **Discovery** — what they're working on, what went wrong last time, what they're afraid of. **The substitute
  already exists and isn't marketing: we are in the specification conversation on most jobs.** It is a better
  listening post than a rep's meeting, because it happens at the moment the room is decided. Today it is used
  only to help; nothing is learned from it systematically. The cheapest unexploited asset in the strategy.
- **Closing, and objections in real time** — Zoom for the considered sale, the store for the self-evident one.
  Content brings a dealer to the point of ordering; something still has to take the order.
- **Product in hand** — the sample kit and the Experience Centre, which is why virtualising that room matters
  (`04-content.md`).
- **Market intelligence** — a rep brings back what competitors are quoting and offering. Nothing in this model
  does that, and we should record it as a genuine hole rather than pretend otherwise.

## What stays human — and it is small enough to staff

- **Wanting the order.** Content cannot want it. Somebody has to notice a dealer has gone quiet, ask for the
  job and follow it up. Thinly covered today, and it is the residue that most deserves a person.
- **The novel problem** — *"I have an impossible project, can you do it?"* The best work the business gets, and
  it should reach an owner. Publishing the routine answers is what keeps that line clear.
- **Recognition and thanks**, which are acts and cannot be published (`03-partner-programme.md`).

## The corpus is the asset — not the channel

The thing being accumulated is a **body of answers**, not a YouTube channel or a blog. Channels are
distribution surfaces; the corpus is what has value, and confusing the two is how content becomes a treadmill.
Four consequences:

- **Publish to owned properties, syndicate to platforms.** The canonical version of an answer lives on our
  sites, indexed and linked; the video also sits on YouTube because that is where it is found. Anything that
  exists *only* on a platform is rented.
- **Platform and personnel risk are not theoretical here.** An older SRND Group YouTube account is locked
  behind an ex-employee's two-factor authentication, which is why the group channel restarted from zero in 2024
  (`04-content.md`). One incident, both risks, and a plain argument for owning the canonical copy.
- **Structure it for retrieval, not for chronology.** A blog is a stream and decays; a knowledge base is a
  reference and accrues. The question a dealer asks is the unit of organisation.
- **It is how the moat stops being person-shaped.** A corpus outlasts anyone's involvement, so building it
  *is* institutionalising the advantage (`00-strategy.md`) rather than a separate project.

## Why it compounds — and the three conditions

Compounding is the whole economic case, so it is worth being exact about what produces it rather than assuming
that publishing enough will do it.

1. **Permanence.** The answer stays up, stays accurate and stays linked. Anything taken down, superseded
   without redirect or left to rot is a cost that decayed rather than an asset that accrued.
2. **Discoverability that improves with depth.** A corpus gets easier to find as it grows — more entry terms,
   more internal linking, more authority on a subject. Each piece makes the others slightly more findable,
   which is the actual mechanism rather than a metaphor.
3. **Reuse.** One recording should become a knowledge-base article, a video, a social hook, a paragraph in a
   datasheet and a slide in a training module. The compounding is as much across surfaces as across time.

**And what compounds is capacity, not revenue.** This is the version that matters given our constraint. Every
question retired stops arriving, which returns owner minutes, which fund the next answer. That is the flywheel
— and it is why the measure is questions retired rather than reach (`04-content.md`). Revenue follows, but
later and less directly than the capacity does.

**The maintenance obligation is the price.** A corpus that compounds is one that has to be kept true: a stale
answer is worse than no answer, because a dealer acts on it. So a periodic review of the highest-traffic
answers is part of the operating cost, not an optional tidy-up. Our own estate already shows the failure —
video product pages for a discontinued carried line and a channel description using naming we have abandoned.

## Why it scales globally — and the four things that limit it

The marginal cost of the next territory is nearly zero, which is the strongest argument for direct-global
(`01-commercial-model.md`). Four real limits, in order of how much they bite:

- **Fulfilment, and this is the binding one.** Content can create demand in places we cannot yet serve well on
  lead time, freight or duty. Awareness that outruns operations produces a bad first order, and the first order
  tests us rather than the product (`02-buyer-journey.md`). So territory expansion is an operations question
  that content should follow, not lead.
- **Language.** The exception that permits appointing a distributor is partly a language barrier — and content
  weakens that exception, because a corpus subtitles and translates far more cheaply than a rep localises.
  **The better the corpus, the fewer territories can justify a distributor**, which is a direct link between
  this document and the 2× test.
- **Local trust.** A dealer wants to know somebody will answer when it matters. Content can't fully supply
  that, but a visible face and a kept promise about response go a long way (`04-content.md`).
- **Time zones**, which the asynchronous layer largely solves — provided it carries as much as possible before
  a synchronous conversation is needed. Every question a dealer can answer at midnight without us is a
  territory served.

**AI answer engines change the arithmetic in our favour.** A deep corpus of published technical answers is
precisely what these systems cite, and no distributor's rep appears in an AI answer. That makes corpus depth
compound faster than it used to, and it is why Share of Model is a metric in this strategy
(`02-buyer-journey.md`) rather than a novelty.

## The handoff: content has to end somewhere

A rep's visit ends with a next step. Most of our content ends with nothing — the video estate has no path to
registration or the store, which quietly wastes whatever reach it earns. The buyer journey's own rule applies
to the corpus: **every stage hands to the next, no dead ends** (`02-buyer-journey.md`).

So each piece carries the next step appropriate to its stage: an explainer points at the fuller answer, the
answer points at the product or the design service, the how-to points at registration, and everything relevant
points at the store. Cheap, mechanical, and currently absent.

## The one thing that must not be scaled

**Authorship.** The advantage is that the answer comes from the people who decided what the product does. Bulk
or generic content — however cheap it has become to produce — destroys precisely that, and it would be
indistinguishable from what any distributor could publish. It is the most tempting shortcut available and the
one that would hollow out the position entirely.

The corollary: **volume is not the goal.** A hundred pieces that answer nothing are worth less than ten that
retire ten questions permanently, and the estate already demonstrates it.

## How far the idea can be pushed

Five extensions, in rough order of leverage:

1. **The rep can have a face.** The literal reading of "make content the rep" is not documents — it is a person
   on screen, and familiarity is the mechanism. Worked through in `04-content.md`; it makes the dormant
   personal-authority channel (`05-channels.md`) the sales motion's most valuable unbuilt asset.
2. **It works for the channel, not only for us.** The long tail of small distributors and dealer-distributors
   has the same sales problem and no depth of its own. Our content can be *their* sales motion — extending
   reach without appointing anybody and without handing over a relationship.
3. **Training is the highest-leverage content there is.** It does the rep's teaching job, justifies the
   registration we ask for, makes the dealer better, and creates real switching cost. Currently unbuilt and
   deferred, which is defensible on capacity and not on value.
4. **Every published answer is permanent.** Content is a compounding asset where a salary is a recurring cost —
   the clearest argument for the model on its own terms.
5. **It is the only affordable route into new territories**, since the alternative — a body in each — is the
   thing that has repeatedly failed here.

## The ceiling, stated plainly

Content can carry a dealer from never having heard of us to ready to order, and can beat any rep we could hire
on authority. It cannot qualify, cannot discover and cannot ask. So the model is not content *instead of*
sales: it is **content doing the reach and the proof, the tools doing the qualification, the design service
doing the discovery, and a very small number of people doing the asking.** Put that way it is a deliberate
design rather than a workaround — and, unlike a field force, it is affordable for the team that exists.
