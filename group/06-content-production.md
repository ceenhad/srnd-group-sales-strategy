# Content production — studio + Experience Centre

Two owned, **group-shared** assets make the strategy affordable and turn the direct-global model
from aspiration into something a small team can actually run. They serve every brand; their output
is brand-specific, their existence is not.

- **The studio** (in-house, for podcast/video) makes rep-quality content cheap to produce at volume
  — the only way to cover every stage of the buyer journey *and* stay always-on (the persistent
  presence that stage 6 depends on). It's also what makes pursuing a new channel affordable: a new
  channel needs re-pointed content, not a new operation. This is why a **content plan is essential**
  per brand: the studio is what lets one small team represent every dealer, everywhere,
  continuously. Output maps straight onto the buyer journey — YouTube explainers (stages 1–2),
  how-to/install video (stages 3, 5), recorded demos (stage 3), podcast (stage 6 presence, and peer
  reach → referral).
- **The Experience Centre** (Kirkbymoorside) is the highest-trust touchpoint, but it's physical and
  scarce — we can't fly a global dealer base to North Yorkshire. So the studio's job is to
  **virtualise it**: film the demos, capture "hear/see the difference," turn the room into content a
  dealer anywhere can experience remotely. Studio and Experience Centre together give a demo you can
  distribute globally — precisely what a direct, no-local-rep model would otherwise lack. This
  pairing may be the single most valuable owned asset in the group.
- **The room-render library** is a third owned asset, already substantial (dozens into the
  hundreds) — but the renders are **raw material, not finished content**. A render on its own is a
  picture: fine, but lazy. **A virtual case study is a *story*** — the room and its brief, the
  problem, what was specified (often across several brands), how it was solved, the result — and the
  render is only the **hook** that draws the reader into that story. Told well, a virtual case study
  is strong proof for stages 2–3, and the natural vehicle for the whole-room, cross-brand story
  (which is why it belongs downstream, not at the hook — `11-intro-campaign.md`). The library means
  the hooks already exist; the work is writing the stories around them.

> **Why this is group-owned.** One studio and one Experience Centre serve all brands. Duplicating
> them per brand would defeat the economics that make the whole direct-global model workable. Brands
> book capacity and define output; they don't own the assets.
