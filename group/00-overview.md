# The group play — in one page

SRND is a **house of brands over one shared store.** Awareness is built per-brand; buying happens
in one place (the portal). The whole commercial operation exists to acquire and compound one thing:
**valuable dealer relationships**, counted across every brand, over years.

**At the centre is the buyer journey** (`03-buyer-journey.md`): from "never heard of us" round to
"orders again" — and, critically, cross-sells into another brand. It is defined once at group level;
each brand runs its own version over a largely shared dealer base.

**Why the group frame changes the economics.** A dealer won for one brand is a natural buyer for
the others, because the brands serve the same room — most of the time a cinema/AV project that pulls
several brands in. So a small order from any one brand is not a low-value account; it's the opening
of a group relationship that compounds. This is why there is no "long tail to service cheaply," and
why direct-to-dealer (not distributor-led) is asset-building, not cost-saving.

**Two things make this affordable for a small team:** the shared **content operation**
(studio + Experience Centre, `06-content-production.md`) that makes rep-quality content cheap at
volume, and the shared **portal** that carries registration, gated pricing and low-friction reorders
in one place for every brand.
