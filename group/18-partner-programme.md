# The partner programme

*What registration grants, and how a valued partner is actually treated. This is the answer to the
appreciation gap named in `14-moat.md` and the policy the on-ramp depends on (`17-on-ramp.md`).
Internal.*

## Where we are

Registration currently grants access to trade pricing, and that is where it stops. There is no partner
programme, no training access, no recognition of any kind. Trade-price gating is established practice in
this business, so it isn't a differentiator — it's the floor. The consequence is the imbalance already
recorded in the moat: the relationship runs one way. Dealers pay, and nothing goes back that tells them
the relationship itself is valued.

The scale of what that overlooks is concrete. One dealer has spent around £500,000 in a year — a
substantial share of group revenue — and there is no mechanism by which they are treated differently
from a first-time account beyond the price on an invoice. That is the gap this closes.

## Two principles

**Spend the depth, not the margin.** Discount is the wrong currency for appreciation. Retro plans and
rebates cost more to administer than they return; a percentage can be matched by any competitor; and
money off is absorbed and forgotten within a week. Worse, it reframes a relationship as a transaction —
it tells a dealer their year is worth a number. What SRND holds that others do not is engineering depth,
a facility, people who genuinely understand these rooms, and end-client demand. Those are scarce,
difficult to copy, and cost very little to share. They are the right currency, and they are the moat
expressed as a programme rather than asserted as a claim.

**No ledgers.** Anything requiring a running balance — points, accrued rebates, retro payment plans — is
excluded by design. Experience says the administration outweighs the value, and disputes over balances
damage the relationship they were meant to reward. Everything here is a **discrete act**: an invitation,
an allocation, an early look, a referral, a gift. Nothing to reconcile, nothing to argue about.

Together these rule out the conventional tiered-discount ladder. That is deliberate, not an omission.

## The three layers

**Registered.** What exists today — trade pricing and a store account — plus the one addition that costs
almost nothing and is owed anyway: **training access**. Training is already required by Pro-Fi, C-ATS has
prior art in tiered dealer certification, and it is the cheapest way to make registration worth the
marketing permission it asks for. It also serves the moat directly: training makes a dealer better at
their job, which is the whole promise.

**Partner** — earned through real, repeat business. This is the access layer, and every element is chosen
because it helps the dealer *sell* rather than because it discounts what they buy:

- certification, and the standing that comes with it;
- a named contact with genuine technical knowledge, rather than a support queue;
- **use of the Experience Centre with their own clients** — our facility helping them close their sale;
- demo and loan stock;
- early sight of new product.

**Top partners** — few, discretionary, and deliberately **not** a published entitlement. Publishing it
would create entitlement and administration, and would cheapen what is meant to be personal. What sits
here is relationship, not schedule:

- involvement in what we build next — a genuine say in the roadmap;
- participation in beta programmes (Pantheon is the immediate example);
- first allocation of new or constrained product;
- **referral of end-client enquiries.** This is the most valuable thing we can give. Sending a dealer
  work makes us a partner rather than a supplier, and no discount imitates it.

## Recognition is an act, not a benefit

For the largest partners the answer is not a line in a benefits table. It is being *known*: knowing what
they spent, saying thank you explicitly and in person, and recognising the people who did the work rather
than only the account that placed the order. Most suppliers never do this at all, which is exactly why it
lands. Prizes and gestures for genuinely good business belong here too — memorable rather than
transactional, and never so gimmicky that they read as a sales promotion.

## One decision that pays twice

Every brand strategy in this repo records the same weakness: real installs, thin case studies, almost no
install photography. Dealers, meanwhile, want recognition and exposure. **Featuring a top partner's work
— professionally photographed and published at our cost — honours them and fills our largest content
gap simultaneously.** One photographer, both sides better off, and the proof assets the content phase
needs (`15-activate.md`) arrive as a by-product of the appreciation programme rather than as a separate
project.

## The discipline

Per the moat: **none of this may be announced until it is real.** Claiming an appreciation we don't yet
practise would cost more than saying nothing. Build it, operate it, then talk about it.

## What still has to be decided

- **The qualifying line for Partner** — what "real, repeat business" means in practice. Keep it
  judgement-based and reviewed periodically rather than an automatic threshold, to avoid the ledger
  problem returning by the back door.
- **Who owns the relationship acts.** Recognition only works if someone is accountable for doing it;
  left to goodwill it won't happen.
- **The training programme itself** — owed to every brand, mandatory for Pro-Fi, and currently
  unbuilt. It is the largest piece of work implied here.
- **Lead referral mechanics** — how end-client enquiries are routed fairly, and how that interacts with
  the direct-sales model (`02-commercial-model.md`).
- **Experience Centre access** — booking, hosting and cost, since dealer-hosted client visits are a real
  demand on the facility and on people's time.
