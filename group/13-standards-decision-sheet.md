# The standards to set — a decision sheet

*The `[decide]` calls from `group/12-task-shapes.md`, collected into a run sheet. Pass 1 found the 19 `varies`
tasks fail on `no standard` — **nobody decided what good looks like** — so these are the decisions that turn the
templates into standards. Each is minutes. Once set, the answer replaces the `[decide]` in the matching shape,
and the task stops varying by whoever does it.*

## How to run it

Same method as the QA: **one at a time, accept the proposed default or override it.** A proposed default is a
**starting point, not a fact** — it is there so the fast answer is "yes, that" and only the ones you'd change
need thought. Where a decision needs your real business truth rather than a sensible guess, it is marked **⚑**
and the proposal is explicitly a draft to correct.

*When answered, each result lands back in `group/12-task-shapes.md`, replacing the `[decide]` in that task's
shape. Nothing here is settled until you say so.*

## Session 1 — the dealer bar *(standard 1 ⚑ · `T-S03`)*

**The question the standard actually has to answer.** If the bar is ability to pay, what is `T-S03`'s verdict
field *for*? The shape requires "a verdict traceable to the criteria" — but a criterion nearly every dealer
passes is not a bar, and the record would be theatre.

### What the evidence already settles

**1. The payment model has already enforced the bar, so holding it at zero costs nothing.** There are **no dealer
credit accounts anywhere, the UK included — payment through the store, and a dealer wanting credit uses a credit
card** (`current-state.md`). A dealer who cannot pay cannot transact. There is no receivable to lose and no bad
debt to underwrite, so *ability to pay* is enforced at checkout rather than by a judgement at approval.

**2. But there is one place credit *is* extended, and it is not the dealer.** Staged payment is normal on
big-ticket carried lines — the Komodo runs at **60 % received**, and part-paid is the ordinary state of a live
project (`current-state.md`). **So payment exposure attaches to a project, not to an account** — which means the
question the reframe dissolved out of `T-S04` is the one place it genuinely bites. Flagged below, not answered.

**3. Triage by size is prohibited by measurement, twice, in two businesses and different decades.** **71 % of
dealers started under £5,000**, that cohort produced **61 % of twelve years' revenue**, the largest account in
twelve years began under £5,000, and the **£1–5k band out-earned the £50k+ band on mean later revenue** (£37,143
against £27,618 — finding 3, replicated in finding 11). The honest form is the prohibition: **half of small
first orders never reorder, so nothing in a first order tells you which half you are looking at.**

**4. So the scarce resource is not capital — it is attention.** The acceptance gate is *already* wide open and
the failure is downstream of it: **348 accounts hold signed T&Cs and pricing access; 189 have never bought**
(finding 27). Admitting more dealers is not the constraint. With no field force, the only attention that exists
is owner minutes and the producer's.

**5. What may rank attention, from the data.** Breadth tracks value monotonically — **multi-line dealers were
44 % of the base and 87.7 % of revenue** (finding 2) — so the surviving ranking signal is *breadth and its
trajectory*, never size. And the live version already exists: the **account × brand signal matrix**
(`14-engine-as-hub.md` §1) — *browsed → quoted → spec'd → ordered → repeat*. **Because the store is fully gated,
every browse is an identified account**, so a dealer reading a second brand's pages is an observable
cross-sell signal months before revenue could show it.

### The proposal

**The verdict collapses into two separate decisions, and only one of them is a bar.**

| | Decision | How it is made | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Admit** | Can they pay? | The single disqualifier; enforced at checkout, confirmed at approval | Set once, at approval |
| **Prioritise** | How much attention, and on which brand? | Computed from signals pulled from engine | Recomputed each period, never set once |

**The qualification record's fields, revised:** **account type** (the five agreed at `14-engine-as-hub.md` §1 —
whole-room integrator · single-brand trade · specifier/consultant · distributor · consumer — set on a screen
already in use); **the payment test**; and the priority inputs, *pulled, never recalled* — brands held, highest
signal tier per brand this period, days since last interaction, and **white space** (a brand browsed or quoted
and never bought).

**Prohibited inputs, and this is the part that has to be written down rather than understood:** first-order
value, order size, and any band derived from either. **A verdict field that encodes size is provably discarding
signal we do not have.**

**Definition of done, revised:** an admit decision traceable to the payment test alone, plus a priority band
traceable to signals pulled from engine and stamped with the date they were pulled. **This is also what makes
the task automatable** — the original shape said a fixed bar is "exactly what makes it automatable," and the
automatable half turns out to be the priority band, not the admit.

### Answered — Neil, 2026-08-15

**Two of the session's assumptions were wrong, and both corrections make the bar sharper rather than looser.**

**1. There *is* a check at approval, and it is a legitimacy test rather than a credit test.** *"We check that
they are VAT registered and a legitimate company based on the IDs they supply."* So the admit test has two
parts, not one: **legitimate registered trade company** (checked at approval, against supplied IDs) **and able
to pay** (enforced at checkout). Both are objective, both already run, and neither involves a judgement about
size — **which is the bar the reframe asked for, already in place.**

**2. Credit *is* available to dealers — just not from us.** *"We do offer them the option to use Iwoca Pay to
get credit — just not from us."* **This corrects `current-state.md`,** which records only that a dealer wanting
credit uses a credit card. The economics are the point: **a third party carries the receivable, so the group
takes no bad-debt exposure while the dealer's ability to pay is materially wider than their cash position.**
That strengthens the reframe again — *"much of our best projects have come from smaller dealers"* is not a
tolerance for risk, because there is no risk to tolerate. **A small dealer with a large project can pay.**

**3. The disqualifiers, named.** Two, beyond failing the legitimacy or payment test:

- **A competitor buying to copy.** Trade access gives sight of drawings, CAD and control detail, and this group
  has a specific reason to guard it (`06-competitors.md`, the Future Automation note).
- **Discretion — *"if we simply decide not to, for historical or other reasons."*** Real, legitimate, and the
  one item here that is not a test. **So it is recorded as a discretionary refusal rather than dressed as a
  rule** — otherwise it quietly becomes one and the next person applies it as though it were.

**Not disqualifiers:** being locked to a competing brand, and reputation as a standalone test. Both were
offered; neither was taken.

### The revised admit test

| Test | How | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| **Legitimate registered trade company** | VAT registration and company identity, verified against supplied IDs at approval | Objective, already running |
| **Able to pay** | Enforced at checkout; **Iwoca Pay offered as a third-party credit route** | Objective, no exposure to us |
| **Not a competitor buying to copy** | Judgement, at approval | Named exclusion |
| **Discretionary refusal** | *"We simply decide not to"* — historical or other reasons | **Logged as discretion, never written as a rule** |

Everything else remains a priority question computed from engine signals, with first-order value and any
size-derived band still prohibited as inputs.

### Still open on this session

   separately? *(The answer above points at the latter.)*
3. **Can priority be overridden by hand, and is the override recorded?**

## Session 2 — the pre-sales / consulting boundary *(standard 2 · `T-S04`, and a new open decision)*

**The question.** Where does free pre-sales end and paid consulting begin — and how is that line crossed inside
a live conversation without breaking `decided.md` B6 (*never insinuate the dealer can't; the design service is
their choice, never a rescue*)?

### What the repo already settles, unassembled

**1. A priced ladder already exists, and it already has a first rung with a number on it.** Free calculators →
the partner-gated C-ATS tool → **Pro Design escalation at £500, invoiced per engagement** → partner-network
escalation → brand-partnered recipe packs → the full design service and verification → **the products that
build the room** (`07-tools.md`). What is missing is not a price list. It is **the same boundary in the human
conversation**, where nothing is priced at all.

**2. The repo already states what is sold, and it is not hours.** *"A deliverable carrying **accountability** —
measured data, judgement, and a result somebody stands behind and can verify. Arithmetic was never the
product"* (`07-tools.md`). So the boundary is accountability, not effort.

**3. The free side is free on purpose, and we are spending money to make it freer.** Content's standard is
*does this answer something that would otherwise land on an owner?* (`04-content.md`). **Everything answerable
from the record is deliberately free** — that is the whole content thesis. So the paid line sits exactly where
the record stops.

**4. And the counter-pressure is real, so name it rather than let it distort the rule.** We are a manufacturer;
**the ladder ends at the room being supplied, not at the report** (`07-tools.md`), and that last rung is the
largest. Charging early can cost the biggest rung. **So the reason to charge is not the fee — it is that
unbounded free consulting spends the one resource the whole strategy is short of.** Same argument as everything
else on this page, which is how you know it is the right one.

### The proposal — one test, three tie-breakers

**The test: free pre-sales is work that becomes content; consulting is work that cannot.**

**Standard 7 already drew this line without anyone noticing it also priced it.** It says capture into `N3` any
answer that isn't already in the record *and could recur* — **not one-off project arithmetic.** That is the same
cut:

- **Reusable → free.** Giving it away once retires the question permanently, which is precisely what the
  content motion is for.
- **One-off project arithmetic → chargeable.** Done for one room, retires nothing, and is paid for by exactly
  one person.

**Three tie-breakers; any one true makes it consulting:**

1. **Bespoke** — the output is specific to this room and could not be published.
2. **Accountable** — we are asked to *stand behind* a result (a specification, a prediction, a verification),
   not to inform a decision the dealer makes. This is the repo's own definition of what is sold.
3. **Sustained** — it needs more than a single exchange, so the cost recurs on our side rather than theirs.

**And free pre-sales carries one obligation, which is already standard 7: capture it.** An answer that was
reusable and never captured is one we paid for twice.

**Crossing the line without breaking B6.** The route is *offered*, priced, with the deliverable named — never
"that's chargeable now." The £500 Pro Design rung is the natural first step: it exists, it is small, and it is
already invoiced per engagement, so the crossing is an existing product rather than an awkward moment.

### Answered — Neil, 2026-08-15

**1. `XS-4` is answered, and the answer is the problem.** *"We have been terrible at working out what is paid
work and what is free pre-sales."* **So today it is neither free nor chargeable by policy — it varies, and it
is not deliberate.** That closes the factual half of `XS-4`: there is no boundary to discover, which is why one
has to be set.

**2. Fixed fee, not a rate.** As proposed, and consistent with the £500 Pro Design rung and with selling the
deliverable rather than the hours.

**3. Charged, and *not* credited against the order.** **This overrides the session's recommendation — and the
override was right.** ~~The session argued that crediting the fee charges for attention without taxing the
manufacturer rung.~~ **Struck by the margin arithmetic** (Neil, 2026-08-15):
**services run at ≈100 % margin against 52.4 % for own-made sold direct**, so **crediting a £3,000 fee converts

**And the same arithmetic changes what this whole decision is worth.** At ≈100 % margin, **free pre-sales is not

**4. Published to dealers.** As proposed — so the boundary is visible before the conversation rather than
announced inside it, which is also what keeps it `B6`-safe.

## Session 3 — the upsell proposal *(standard 3 · `T-S05`, a build item)*

**The question.** How should a proposal be presented to maximise the upsell?

### The distinction the session has to make first, or it gets the timing wrong

**There are two cross-sell mechanisms and they fire at different moments.**

- **Dealer-level cross-sell — the second *line* an account takes.** Median **246 days** later; **74 %** not in
  the opening basket (finding 4). Fires on an established account; it is a `detect` rule, and
  `adjacency-map.md` already carries it as a standing rule.
- **Room-level attach — the layers of *this* room, being decided now.** That is what a proposal is, and finding
  4 says nothing about it.

**Confusing the two would delay the proposal upsell by eight months for no reason.**

**And there is a measured reason to fix the proposal.** **£472,320 of Leyard Komodo LED against £38,452 of
own-made Screen Wall** — every Komodo sale is a room being built, and the attach did not happen (finding 14/22;
`NEXT.md` lane 9). `14-engine-as-hub.md` §1 Q5 already names **own-made attach rate on carried sales** as the
bridge metric. **So this standard is the instrument that moves a named KPI, and the KPI already has a
baseline.**

### The proposal design — six rules, each traceable

1. **The unit is the room, not the enquiry.** Sections are the room's layers in build order — structure and
   front-of-room, treatment, surface, audio, light and control — with the enquiry sitting *in its layer* rather
   than at the top.
2. **Every layer appears, priced or reasoned. The form has no blank cell.** Each layer carries one of four
   statuses: **specified** · **offered** (a priced increment) · **not applicable to this room** (with the
   reason) · **not ours** (said plainly — Light Walls deferred, Pro-Fi pre-shipping). This is `T-S05`'s existing
   rule — *a flagged gap, never a silent omission* — applied to layers instead of fields.
3. **The justification is physical, never commercial.** `adjacency-map.md` already supplies the sentence for
   every pair — *"panels install behind the fabric wall"*, *"screens, port holes, LED and vents are set into the
   fabric surface"*. A proposal that says *you may also like* has left the register.
4. **The increment is priced inside the same document.** One relationship means one document; a second proposal
   is the fragmentation the group exists to remove. *(Partner-facing and gated, so this is consistent with the
   no-public-pricing rule.)*
5. **The upsell paragraph is written as the dealer's win, never our range** — one order, one delivery, drawings
   that fit, integration already known. `CLAUDE.md`'s dealer-is-the-hero rule, and also the only honest reason a
   dealer should take the layer from us rather than from anyone else.
6. **It passes the honesty gate before it goes** — the brand's `G2` (session 6), no superlative without a
   measurement, pre-shipping stated plainly, no invented figures.

**Output:** fixed sections per layer, each assembled from the record's on-ramp fields (`D1`, `O1`–`O4`, `H2`)
plus the adjacency justification and a status — and a closing **"not included, and why"** section, which is what
makes rule 2 visible to the dealer rather than only to us.

**Definition of done:** every layer of the room carries one of the four statuses; every *offered* layer carries
its physical justification and its price; gaps flagged; gate passed.

**Acceptance test**, per §1's rule that each new thing inherits a baseline: **own-made attach rate on carried
sales**, against the £472,320 / £38,452 split as it stands today.

> **▲ Amended 2026-08-15 — the attach metric was aimed one rung too low.** At ≈100 % margin,
> **service attach earns about twice as much per pound as own-made attach and needs no product to exist**
>. It also already happens: **Leyard Gold-tier commissioning at
> £5,040–£6,000 against Komodo orders.** So on a £472,320 Komodo year **the best attach is not a Screen Wall —
> it is commissioning and calibration on every one**, and the Screen Wall is the second ask rather than the
> first. **Track service attach and own-made attach as co-equal metrics**, and note the consequence for rule 2:
> the room's layers are not only products. **A layer's *service* — design, commissioning, verification — is an
> offerable line in the proposal, and currently never appears in one.**

### Answered — Neil, 2026-08-15

**An unrequested layer is shown as a priced increment.** As proposed. So rule 2's *offered* status carries a
number, in the same document, and the upsell is takeable rather than a further enquiry.

**One consequence to carry into the build:** a priced increment for a layer whose record is empty is a number
we cannot stand behind. **So the proposal's completeness rule and the record's fill state are now coupled** —
where `product-register.md` shows a gap, the proposal either prices it anyway or drops to *named, ask*, and
that inconsistency is exactly what rule 2 exists to prevent. **Pricing an increment is therefore also an
argument for finishing the record** (step 3), which is the plan's critical path regardless.

### ▲ Re-scoped by Neil, 2026-08-16 — two artefacts, two stages, and one design killed

**What goes out today is a store quote** (`Q9`), and the quote is not a weak proposal — **it is a mechanical
document with a contractual job**: *"it allows a customer to formally accept our offer before it goes to
invoice."* **The proposal is a separate, earlier, sales-stage artefact.** The chain Neil describes:

> **proposal → accepted (by the salesperson or the dealer) → formal quote → invoice** — *"engine stores sales
> interactions by project, so it would be possible to have a separate proposal"* at the head of it.

**So the upsell has two possible homes, and the cheap one is not the one this session designed.**

| | Where | What it is | Cost to build |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Optional lines on the quote** | *"That mechanical quote can also contain optional 'have you also considered' type line items that would be added to the invoice"* | **Small** — a field on an artefact that already exists and already flows to invoice |
| **B** | **The proposal** | The sales-stage document, earlier and persuasive rather than contractual | **A build** — a new object in engine, per-project |

**Take A first.** It attaches the increment at the moment of formal acceptance, it needs no new document, and it
is the direct mechanical answer to the attach gap — **a quote lists what was asked for, so a layer
nobody requested has nowhere to appear.** £472,320 of Komodo against £38,452 of Screen Wall is what that
produces.

### And the constraint that kills this session's original design

> *"The process of making the proposal has to be **quick and easy**, and is a loop we have [been] round a dozen
> times. At one time we even had **three graphic designers on staff producing per-product blocks to drop into
> design-based proposals. Doesn't work.** **Speed of delivery of proposals is just as important as content**,
> quite often."*

~~**This session proposed a document assembled from record fields, per layer. That is per-product blocks
dropped into a template — already tried with three people on staff, and failed. Struck as a design.**~~

> **▲ Struck the wrong thing — corrected by Neil, same day.** ***"The content blocks thing works… if you have
> the content blocks!"*** **The failure was never assembly. It was that the blocks were being *produced*, by
> hand, by three designers, against each proposal.** *Assembly is cheap; authoring per proposal is what cost
> three salaries and did not work.* **So block assembly is the right design — it just has a precondition
> nobody had met.**

**Which turns the proposal into two deliverables, and neither is a document-design exercise:**

| | Deliverable | What it is | Where it rides |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The block library** | ***"All of our products and services are going to have to be listed in there."*** One block per product and **per service** — the presentation form of what the record already holds. *"To add a block seems achievable"* | **On the record** (step 3). A block is generated from a filled record, not authored beside it — the same argument as the record's own fields |
| **2** | **A document template that consumes blocks** | ***"That then needs a document template that can use it."*** The thing that turns a room's block set into a sent proposal | A build, and the smaller of the two once the library exists |

**And this closes the loop between sessions 1 and 3, which were being worked as separate problems.** **A service
cannot have a block until it is defined and costed** — Neil's own rule from `Q34`, arriving from the other
direction. **So session 1 (the service offer) is a hard dependency of the proposal build**, not a neighbour of
it: *undefined services cannot appear in a proposal, which is exactly why the attach never happens.*

**Speed survives as a first-class requirement, and it is now achievable rather than aspirational.** *Time from
"we should propose" to "it is sent"* is the acceptance test alongside attach rate — **and the whole point of a
maintained library is that the marginal proposal costs minutes.** The rule that follows: **the per-proposal
cost must be assembly only. Any design that puts authoring inside the proposal loop rebuilds the thing that
already failed.**

2. **Should the layer list be fixed per room type** — cinema, media room, commercial fit-out, hospitality? It

## Session 4 — daily per brand *(standard 10 · `T-M06`)*

**The question.** *"If you take all the content needed it's daily per brand."* What production line serves that,
what does it need, and what does it cost?

### Size it honestly first

**Four viable brands** — DT, Fabric Walls, C-ATS, Pro-Fi (`NEXT.md`'s tiers) — **at one weekday piece each is
20 a week, roughly 1,000 a year.**

**Against the measured estate: 109 videos across three channels in eight years, 54,500 lifetime views**
(`04-content.md`). **So daily-per-brand is not a cadence increase. It is a different machine** — and saying so
plainly is what stops it being read as a stretch target on the current process.

### The decomposition, which is what makes it feasible

**The constraint that must not break is that owner minutes stay at the answer step.** So the line's input is
*answers*, and the rate is a product of two independent levers with two different owners:

> **rate = answers per period × derivatives per answer**

- **Answers** come from batch recording (`04-content.md`), the **archive backfill** (years of sent mail as a
  retrospective question log — lane 6's smallest next move, still undone), the **inbound log**
  (`14-engine-as-hub.md` §1) and the **three-line spec capture** (`adjacency-map.md`). **Three of those four are
  already asked for elsewhere in the plan**, so the answer supply needs no new mechanism — only the ones already
  named.
- **Derivatives** come from the production line reading the record: a knowledge-base article, a short video, a
  social hook, a datasheet paragraph, a newsletter item. This is `assemble` (`09-motion-design.md`), and
  `04-content.md` already describes the multiple.

**The arithmetic, marked as hypothesis:** *10 answers per recording session × 5 derivatives per answer = 50
pieces ≈ two and a half weeks of daily-per-brand.* **So roughly two sessions a month sustains it.** Both numbers
are guesses to be replaced by the first real batch — **the derivative multiple in particular is unmeasured
here** — but the shape is what matters: the target is reachable at two owner sessions a month, or it is not
reachable at all.

### Two rules the line needs, both drawn from what we have already measured

**1. Every piece must make an argument.** The estate's own numbers say the winners explain or show —
**a 52-second reverberation explainer at ~9,500 views, DT mechanisms at 600–1,500** — and the catalogue fails:
**two dozen competent product films between 1 and 26 views.** A derivative that neither explains nor shows *is*
the catalogue again, at machine speed. **The characteristic failure of a production line is producing the thing
already measured not to work, faster.**

**2. The answer carries the face; the derivative carries the pointer.** Familiarity is the mechanism
(`04-content.md`, "the rep has a face") and **nothing in eight years and 54,500 views has one**. Derivatives
cannot supply it. So the recorded original is the piece with a person in it, and everything downstream links
back to it — which also keeps the derivative honest: a route to the answer, not a substitute for it.

### And a correction to how the floor should be expressed

**Set the floor in answers per month, not publications per week.** Publications are the *system's* output and
the system's promise; answers are the owner's. A floor stated in publications puts the owner on the hook for a
number the production line is supposed to deliver — which is exactly how this lane stalls. **The floor decision
(still Neil's and Olivier's) becomes answerable once it is asked in the right unit.**

### Answered — Neil, 2026-08-15

**Scope: the four viable brands.** DT, Fabric Walls, C-ATS, Pro-Fi — so ~20 pieces a week, as sized above.
Light Walls has nothing that can honestly be said yet and the carried lines have no brand voice.

**And the floor question was answered by rejecting its unit — correctly.** *"I'm pretty efficient at the making
part — it's the planning that makes videos difficult. With a plan and script you can bash out loads in a
day."*

> **~~Correction to this session's own proposal.~~** ~~It argued the floor should be set in **answers per
> month**, on the grounds that answers are the owner's unit and publications are the system's.~~ **Struck. The
> owner's output is not the constraint at all** — given a plan and a script it is high and cheap. **The
> constraint sits entirely upstream of the owner, in the planning.** So a floor expressed as anything the owner
> produces measures the wrong thing.

**The floor belongs to the production line, and its unit is scripted briefs delivered to the owner.** Two
commitments, two owners:

| Who | Commits to | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| **The producer / the line** | Putting scripted, ranked briefs in front of the owner | **Briefs per period — this is the floor** |
| **The owner** | Recording what arrives | **A booked slot, not a quantity** |

**This is the strongest confirmation yet of a rule `04-content.md` already carries** — *"the owner never faces
a blank page… correcting a ranked draft costs minutes, inventing a list costs hours"* — arriving this time from
the owner rather than from the argument. **And it re-points the whole of session 4:** the production line's
hard part is not generating derivatives, it is **generating briefs good enough to record from.** That is a
record-and-ranking job, which is step 3 again.

**And "script" means a beat sheet — settled 2026-08-15.** What to cover, in order, and what not to say; the
owner talks around it. **So the two positions were the same thing under two words**, and `04-content.md`'s
*unscripted enough to be human* stands unamended — the thing that makes the format work is the thing that makes
it fast.

**Which finally makes the brief specifiable, and it is a small artefact:** the points to cover in order, the
things not to say (the brand's `G2`, session 6), and any figure that has to be exact. **That is generable from
the record** — which is the whole argument of step 3 arriving at the content lane from a third direction.

### ⚠ This whole session rests on a concept that was already struck — found 2026-08-16

**There is no floor rate, and there has not been one since 2026-07-31.** `decided.md` **S24**: *"**No cadence
ceremony: no set floor rate**, no formal role confirmations."* And the Reversals entry says it twice: *"the

### Light Walls *(deferred — and the honest `G2` is short, but no longer empty)*

**▲ But two hard don'ts now exist and bind from the first public word** — LWCP's freedom-to-operate rests on
them (reviewed read-only in the LWCP repo):

- **Never claim optical feedback in the colour loop.** No photodetector, no LED-as-sensor, no optical sensing
  of the light's *own output* at runtime — deliberately, and it is what clears a competitor portfolio **in
  force to 2034**. Correction runs **open-loop from a stored measured profile** plus thermal and electrical
  telemetry.
- **Never describe the gamut as tessellated into three-coordinate mixing zones.** The design is **a single RGB
  triangle plus a free W channel**, and the distinction is what clears a second patent.

> **⚠ Correction, 2026-08-15 — the first draft of this entry got it dangerously wrong.** It read *"never claim
> optical sensing"*, which would deny a real capability. **LWCP has a ratified spatial-sensing layer** —
> mmWave, time-of-flight depth, UWB, audio and environment, camera-free by posture, under ADRs 0044–0047.
> **The ban is narrow and specific: optical feedback on the luminaire's own output inside the colour loop.**
> Presence, position and environment sensing are **capability to describe, not a claim to avoid.** *Recorded
> rather than silently edited, because a guardrail that over-reaches suppresses real work, and this one nearly
> did.*

**Says instead, when it says anything:** *measured per-unit, and held on target for life.* **These are patent
constraints wearing brand-guardrail clothing** — a careless marketing sentence here is a legal problem rather
than a tone problem, which makes this the one `G2` list where the stakes are not reputational.
