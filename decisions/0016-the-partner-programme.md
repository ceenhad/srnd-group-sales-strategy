# ADR 0016 — Spend the depth, not the margin; no ledgers; and the differentiator is owning the factory

- **Status:** **Proposed** — 2026-08-16. **Not accepted.** Only Neil moves this to `accepted` (ADR 0001 §3).
- **Source:** `proposals.md`, consolidated. **This is a sort, not a rewrite** — the decisions below are as they
  were recorded, with their original evidence. *Nothing has been added; where the original was thin it stays
  thin.*

## Context

Registration grants trade pricing and stops there, so the relationship runs one way: dealers pay, and
nothing goes back that says the relationship is valued. One dealer has spent around £500,000 in a year
and nothing distinguishes their treatment from a first-time account beyond the price on an invoice.

## Decision

**1. Spend the depth, not the margin. No ledgers** — every element is a discrete act. *Discount is the wrong
currency for appreciation: a percentage can be matched by anyone, and it reframes a relationship as a
transaction. Anything requiring a running balance — points, accrued rebates, retro plans — is excluded by
design.*

**2. Distributor services are our baseline, not our differentiator.** We have been a distributor for twenty
years; credit, logistics, reps, demo rooms and training are the day job. *A correction of an earlier
misjudgement that read the parity as a threat.*

**3. The differentiator is owning the brands, the factory and what the products do** — changing the product is
the thing no distributor can do.

**4. The named technical contact is bounded**, or it rebuilds the trap that made field sales fail: every
question routing to the scarce person.
