The jobs — what a sales and marketing rep is for

1 Attract Make the solution as attractive as it honestly is. Every hook comes from here.
2 Defend Hold it against the alternatives — including the dealer's incumbent supplier, and doing nothing.
3 Build trust Be believed. The proof, the honest limits, and the claims we refuse to make — the wording boundary that stops a writer saying something untrue or unsupported.
4 Deliver Every question answered clearly, all the way to a working install.

list 3 of 4 · twelve domains

The domains — the twelve things a dealer asks about

Each domain is a question a dealer asks. A task above fails if we cannot answer it. These become the fields of the product record.

“What is it?” — PhysicalConstruction, materials, finishes, sizes, weights, what arrives in the box
“What does it do, and how well?” — PerformanceThe function, the mechanism, the measured numbers, the test they came from, and the limits
“Where does it go, and how many do I need?” — ApplicationWhich rooms and which positions, the sizing rule, the quantity, a worked example, and where it does not belong
“What does it need from everything else?” — IntegrationPower, structure, clearance, what the builder must leave, and what it pairs with — ours and other people's
“Why this one and not theirs?” — CompetitiveThe named alternatives, what decides between them, where we win, where they win, and what to say to a dealer who already buys the other
“What does it cost me to sell and fit?” — CommercialOrder unit, lead time, availability, margin position, the labour it takes on site, and what skipping it costs. Never the price
“How do I know it works?” — EvidenceTest reports, reference rooms, demonstrations, renders — and which of them we are allowed to show
“What could go wrong?” — RiskThe doubt behind the question, the failure modes on site, and what a mistake actually costs
The questions we actually get asked — AnswersEach one written down, answered in the answerer's own words, with where the answer is published
“What happens after I order it?” — LifecycleDelivery, install sequence, commissioning, spares, what can be replaced, and how long we support it
“What is interesting enough to lead with?” — Hook materialWhich of the five appeals this product can honestly carry, and the substance behind each
“What are we allowed to say?” — GovernanceClaims supported and by what, claims we refuse to make, whether it is released yet, and when the record was last checked
list 4 of 4 · six routes

The routes — when each thing gets written down

A route is a rule: when this happens, this gets written down. Five of the six triggers already happen every week. The work is being done either way; only the writing-down is new.

RT1When a product is made or changedWrite down what it is, what it is supplied with, what it needs from the building, how it goes inper change
RT2When a test result or report arrivesWrite down the measured performance, the test basis, and what may be shown as proofrarely
RT3When we help specify a jobWrite down how you choose it and how many, the layout, and what it pairs withdaily
RT4When we answer a question — pre-sale or on siteWrite down the question itself, the answer in the answerer's own words, and what went wrongdaily
RT5When we quote, and when we win or loseThe commercial case, and which competitor beat us on which dimensionengine CRM
RT6When a position is settled or reviewedWrite down what may and may not be claimed, and what a product can honestly be hooked onthe one new habit

RT5 already runs in engine's CRM — classic pipeline management, so quotes, stages and win/loss are recorded today. One thing left to check with engine: does the loss reason record which competitor and which dimension, or only that the deal was lost?

supporting · four roles

The roles — four hats, not four people

Everyone here wears more than one. A role is a mode of work, not a job title. The same person switches between them in a day. Naming them separates the kinds of work, not the staff.

Owner — deciding what is true and what may be saidConfirming a claim is accurate today, approving a ranked list, saying what may be published. Never authoring the list. A gate, not a job: minutes at a time, in batches.
Authority — supplying the technical answer, in their own voiceThe answer a dealer believes because it comes from whoever designed the thing. On camera where a piece needs a face. The scarce input — spend it on substance, never on production.
Relationship — being the human the dealer deals withThe welcome, the walkthrough, the invitation, the follow-up — and noticing a dealer has gone quiet and asking. Anything that ends in a conversation.
Producer — getting it out and keeping it currentDeciding what needs discussing, briefing it, publishing it, owning the record. Deliberately off camera: knowing what to discuss is the scarcer skill.

Two combinations to watch. Authority and producer in one person: if whoever holds the knowledge also owns publishing, it waits on the busiest person and stops. The strategy already rules against this one. Owner and producer in one person: the truth-check and the deadline sit in one head, so things go out unchecked or do not go out.

the strategy · the hinge

Registration splits the job in two

One moment turns an unknown contact into a potential partner: they register and give permission to market to them. Everything arranges around that boundary, because it is the point at which what we are allowed to do changes.

what we are allowed to do, either side of registration
before — anonymousBrand-led, public, attraction only. No permission and no relationship, so nothing can be direct and nothing can be a group pitch. The brands do it in their own categories. Progress is a funnel count
registration
after — known and permissionedGroup can act directly. One account covers every brand. Progress is tracked per account — journey state, last touch, next action
Before the hinge — brand-led, public, attraction onlyNo permission and no relationship, so it cannot be direct and cannot be a group pitch. The brands do it in their own categories: sites, content, channels, search and AI visibility
The hinge — registration is the conversion that mattersNot the first order. It should be the primary metric of the on-ramp, and it has to be worth something to the dealer so permission is given willingly rather than extracted
After the hinge — partner development, permissionedNow the group can act directly, and progress is tracked per account rather than as a funnel count
the strategy · six gateways

The gateways between stages

A gateway is the state change between two stages. A process needs three things at each: the change itself, a signal we can observe, and the touch that moves a dealer through.

G1The hook landed — a stranger with a problem found us and lookedSignal: a brand-site visit arriving from a hook — search, an AI answer, social, press, a referral1 → 2
G2The proposition landed — “could this work for me?” became worth their timeSignal: depth engagement — knowledge-base reads, a tool used, a download taken, a sample or enquiry requested2 → 3
G3Convinced enough to commit — registration, the hingeSignal: a registration in engine. The one gateway already formally defined and instrumented3 → 4
G4The first order placed — the relationship becomes realSignal: an order in engine4 → 5
G5The first job succeeded — the install went right and the dealer looked goodSignal: the weakest today — an uneventful order, a reorder, a verification completed. Needs defining5 → 6
G6Recalled and expanded — the next project lands and a second layer of the room is oursSignal: an order from a second brand6 → loop

G3, G4 and G6 are cleanly observable today. G2's signals partly exist. G5 needs defining before anything can trigger on it.

the strategy · five components

The machinery that serves the journey

Designed once at group level; every brand inherits the design and supplies its own substance. No brand forks the loop, sets its own rate, or runs its own version.

five components, and the stages each one serves
component 1The answerA question answered once, properly, by whoever knowsevery stage
component 2The production lineSubstance in, drafted at volume, truth-checked in minutes, distributedevery stage
component 3The faceFamiliarity doing what a rep's repeat visits used to dostages 1–3
component 4Being foundSearch, AI answers, channels, trade pressstage 1 · G1
component 5The substitutesWhat content cannot do, each owned by namestages 2–6
1The answer — the unit of the motionA question answered once, properly, by whoever knows. Everything else is a derivative of it. Every piece carries its next step
2The operation — a production line, not a ceremonySubstance in → drafted at volume → truth-checked in minutes → distributed. The labour moves; the judgement stays. Volume of pieces, never volume of assertions
3The face — familiarity doing what repeat visits didA dealer feels they know a presenter they watch regularly. Frequency and regularity beat production value; same face, same room, same frame
4Being found — reach without a bodySearch, AI answers, the channels and the trade press. Content is what gets knocked on, in territories we will never have a person in
5The substitutes — what content cannot do, owned by nameQualification → the tools. Discovery → the spec conversation. Closing → a Zoom or the store. Wanting the order → a person. Market intelligence → a recorded hole
the strategy · five appeals

How a hook is categorised

The same product bites differently for different dealers, so hooks are grouped by appeal. Field H1 records which of these a given product can honestly carry — and marking one empty matters as much as filling it.

Better resultsThe client experiences the difference

the strategy · standing rules

The disciplines that bind everything above

Coherence, not inventionThe substance is real and already exists. The job is to surface what is genuinely there and make one true story flow group → brand → product, not to manufacture claims
Build it, then say itWhere the group has the intent but not yet the reality, the reality comes first and the claim waits. Announcing something we do not yet practise costs more than silence
Lead with the dealer's win, never our own brillianceDepth spent on the dealer reads as generosity; depth asserted reads as arrogance. The dealer is the hero, always
Flag, don't guessWhere a fact is not sourced from real jobs or a settled decision, leave it out and mark it. No invented personas, figures or dates
Restraint over superlativesSubstance carried in plain language, not genericised adjectives. No superlative without a measurement behind it. Accept being underestimated on first read
Pricing is never publicRegistered-partner-only, fully gated — no public prices and no end-user prices. Public claims stay qualitative: performance, depth, measured data
Don't fence the group to cinema — in tradeCinema is the heartland of revenue and credibility and should lead where it is strongest, but none of the underlying competences are cinema-specific. Name the problem, never the room type. The reverse holds in consumer, where the proposition stays DIY home cinema deliberately
Engine owns operationsPartner registrations, pricing, jobs, the CRM. The strategy owns the mechanics and checks engine before specifying anything that touches it
