Jobs, tasks, domains, routes

The four lists the approach is built from. A rep does four jobs. To do them they perform 46 tasks. To perform those they need to know twelve things. Those twelve get filled by six routes. The product record, the form and the register all follow from these four lists.

Every row is a claim, not a finding. Assembled from the strategy documents, not from watching anyone work. Verdicts and performers are proposals: strike what is wrong. One verification pass has been done — nine verdicts changed and one task was added.

The jobs — what a sales and marketing rep is for

1 Attract Make the solution as attractive as it honestly is. Every hook comes from here.
2 Defend Hold it against the alternatives — including the dealer's incumbent supplier, and doing nothing.
3 Build trust Be believed. The proof, the honest limits, and the claims we refuse to make — the wording boundary that stops a writer saying something untrue or unsupported.
4 Deliver Every question answered clearly, all the way to a working install.

list 3 of 4 · twelve domains

The domains — the twelve things a dealer asks about

Each domain is a question a dealer asks. A task above fails if we cannot answer it. These become the fields of the product record.

01“What is it?” — PhysicalConstruction, materials, finishes, sizes, weights, what arrives in the box
02“What does it do, and how well?” — PerformanceThe function, the mechanism, the measured numbers, the test they came from, and the limits
03“Where does it go, and how many do I need?” — ApplicationWhich rooms and which positions, the sizing rule, the quantity, a worked example, and where it does not belong
04“What does it need from everything else?” — IntegrationPower, structure, clearance, what the builder must leave, and what it pairs with — ours and other people's
05“Why this one and not theirs?” — CompetitiveThe named alternatives, what decides between them, where we win, where they win, and what to say to a dealer who already buys the other
06“What does it cost me to sell and fit?” — CommercialOrder unit, lead time, availability, margin position, the labour it takes on site, and what skipping it costs. Never the price
07“How do I know it works?” — EvidenceTest reports, reference rooms, demonstrations, renders — and which of them we are allowed to show
08“What could go wrong?” — RiskThe doubt behind the question, the failure modes on site, and what a mistake actually costs
09The questions we actually get asked — AnswersEach one written down, answered in the answerer's own words, with where the answer is published
10“What happens after I order it?” — LifecycleDelivery, install sequence, commissioning, spares, what can be replaced, and how long we support it
11“What is interesting enough to lead with?” — Hook materialWhich of the five appeals this product can honestly carry, and the substance behind each
12“What are we allowed to say?” — GovernanceClaims supported and by what, claims we refuse to make, whether it is released yet, and when the record was last checked

The routes — when each thing gets written down

A route is a rule: when this happens, this gets written down. Five of the six triggers already happen every week. The work is being done either way; only the writing-down is new.

R1When a product is made or changedWrite down what it is, what it is supplied with, what it needs from the building, how it goes inper change
R2When a test result or report arrivesWrite down the measured performance, the test basis, and what may be shown as proofrarely
R3When we help specify a jobWrite down how you choose it and how many, the layout, and what it pairs withdaily
R4When we answer a question — pre-sale or on siteWrite down the question itself, the answer in the answerer's own words, and what went wrongdaily
R5When we quote, and when we win or loseThe commercial case, and which competitor beat us on which dimensionengine CRM
R6When a position is settled or reviewedWrite down what may and may not be claimed, and what a product can honestly be hooked onthe one new habit

R5 already runs in engine's CRM — classic pipeline management, so quotes, stages and win/loss are recorded today. One thing left to check with engine: does the loss reason record which competitor and which dimension, or only that the deal was lost?

supporting · four roles

The roles — four hats, not four people

Everyone here wears more than one. A role is a mode of work, not a job title. The same person switches between them in a day. Naming them separates the kinds of work, not the staff.

—Owner — deciding what is true and what may be saidConfirming a claim is accurate today, approving a ranked list, saying what may be published. Never authoring the list. A gate, not a job: minutes at a time, in batches.
—Authority — supplying the technical answer, in their own voiceThe answer a dealer believes because it comes from whoever designed the thing. On camera where a piece needs a face. The scarce input — spend it on substance, never on production.
—Relationship — being the human the dealer deals withThe welcome, the walkthrough, the invitation, the follow-up — and noticing a dealer has gone quiet and asking. Anything that ends in a conversation.
—Producer — getting it out and keeping it currentDeciding what needs discussing, briefing it, publishing it, owning the record. Deliberately off camera: knowing what to discuss is the scarcer skill.

Two combinations to watch. Authority and producer in one person: if whoever holds the knowledge also owns publishing, it waits on the busiest person and stops. The strategy already rules against this one. Owner and producer in one person: the truth-check and the deadline sit in one head, so things go out unchecked or do not go out.
