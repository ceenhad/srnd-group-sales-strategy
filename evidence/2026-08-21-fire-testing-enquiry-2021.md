# The 2021 fire-testing enquiry, read with its five attachments — 2026-08-21

`Acoustic_Panel_Fire_Testing.msg`, Neil Davidson to `alastair.monk@italabs.co.uk`, 5 March 2021, supplied by Neil.
An enquiry to a test lab, with five attachments: the two IMO FTP Code annexes Lloyd's requires, and manufacturer
sheets for three of the four materials.

**It is the enquiry, and the reply killed it.** Alastair Monk, ITA Labs, 9 March 2021, four days later, in full:
*"Whilst we are able to carry out the UL-94 test that you request, we are not a Certified Test House and are not able
to issue formal test certificates. You would need a test house certified by Underwriters Laboratories."*

Two things about that reply. It answers only on **UL-94** and never engages with the IMO FTP Code tests that were the
actual request. And ITA Labs is the **International Tin Association** — a materials lab, not a fire certification
body. So the enquiry did not fail on the product; it went to the wrong kind of organisation and stopped there.

**Neil, 2026-08-21: it was never done.** Five years on, there is no marine fire test for any C-ATS panel.

## What it settles: the construction of all three standard panels

Stated by the owner to a third party, in writing, in 2021.

| Panel | Size given | Base material |
|---|---|---|
| Reverberation | 300 × 300 × 50 mm | Pyrosorb foam |
| Reflection | 300 × 300 × 50 mm | Starex ABS |
| Resonance | 600 × 600 × **43 mm** | Laminate — Perspex front surface, acoustic foam rear |

Three things follow. **The Reflection panel's material is answered** — the open `[?]` in the record's new Construction
field. **The 43 mm is independently confirmed two years before `Q82`**, told to a test lab, which closes any remaining
doubt that BSRIA's 50 mm was a logged nominal. And the Resonance panel is **Perspex-faced**: the plate is the front
surface presented to the room, foam behind it, which is what makes it a resonator rather than an absorber.

All four material names are supplier or brand names and never appear in public content.

## The fire position is not one missing number. It is four different positions

`T1` and `DAT-3`/`DOC-7` have been carried as "blocked on the current EN 13501-1 classification". The pack shows the
question is materially wider, because every panel is a different material.

| Material | What the attached sheet actually says |
|---|---|
| Starex ABS VH-0800 E (Reflection) | **UL 94 V-0** at a 1.5 mm specimen. Flame-retardant grade. No BS or EN building classification stated. Sheet last updated April 2006 |
| Pyrosorb-S (Reverberation) | **No fire data at all.** Two pages: thermal conductivity, and absorption coefficients. The covering note says the manufacturer certificate is attached for it; the attached sheet is thermal and acoustic |
| Perspex cast acrylic (Resonance front face) | **BS 476 Part 7 Class 3. UL 94 HB.** And verbatim: *"PERSPEX® is a combustible material and if ignited will continue to burn. However, unlike many other plastic materials, burning PERSPEX® produces very little smoke"* |
| The Resonance panel's rear foam | **No manufacturer sheet.** Selected on ratings the enquiry lists: BS 3379 combustion-modified slabstock, nominal classification A; Schedule 1 Part 1 of the Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety) Amended Regulations 1989; capable of UL 94 HF1 and FMVSS 302 |

**Two of these need attention and one is a live claim.**

The knowledge base states "the C-ATS Reverberation Control Panel uses a Class O acoustic foam core." **The fire
certificate for that foam is not in this pack** — what is attached for it carries no fire data. The claim may be
evidenced elsewhere; on this evidence it is not, and it is the only product fire claim the content makes.

And the Resonance panel's front face is **Class 3 under BS 476 Part 7 and combustible by its own manufacturer's
wording**. Class 0 is the top of that scale. The mitigating fact is real and already in the record — the panels sit
behind a stretch-fabric finish, so the surface exposed to the finished room is the fabric — but **the record says
nowhere that one panel's face is the weak link on a reaction-to-fire question**, and that is precisely the question
`T1` exists to answer. The knowledge base's own caveat is doing the right work: *"confirm the current certificate for
your specific specification rather than relying on a general class"*, with the finish named as part of the assembly.

## What it settles about marine, precisely

The requirement is **Lloyd's Register**, and the tests are named: **IMO FTP Code Annex 1 Part 2 (Smoke and Toxicity)**
and **Annex 1 Part 5 (Test for Surface Flammability)**, both attached. Neil's own framing: *"it has been difficult for
me to get a definitive statement on what is actually required… the key requirement is actually to meet the Lloyds
Register requirements to ensure that vessel can be signed off."*

This is far sharper than the "IMO/SOLAS" the content uses, and it is what a specifier on a yacht project will ask for.

**And it reframes the marine product and `T1` with it.** In 2021 the intent was to certify the three standard panels
for marine use. That attempt stopped at the first reply and was not resumed. Today the marine offer is a separate
hand-made panel in a different material (`Q83`), sold "only in very specific cases under demand from Lloyd's".

So the marine leg of `T1` has been carried as *we hold the compliance evidence and dealers need teaching*. On this
evidence **there is no panel-level marine fire certificate at all**. The plausible reading — and it is only that until
an owner says so — is that compliance rests on the **solid-surface material's own marine approval** rather than on a
test of the assembled panel, which would be why the marine variant is a different material rather than a different
finish on the same one. `DOC-41`.

## One aside worth keeping

Pyrosorb-S's manufacturer sheet publishes the foam's own absorption coefficients. At 50 mm, BS EN 20354:
0.24 · 0.47 · 0.75 · 0.84 · 0.87 · 1.02 from 125 Hz to 4 kHz. The BSRIA figures for the finished Reverberation panel:
0.25 · 0.51 · 0.88 · 1.09 · 1.07 · 1.18. Two independent tests broadly agreeing is worth having. It also says plainly
that the Reverberation panel's value is not a proprietary material — anyone can buy the foam — which is the strongest
possible argument for the position this brand already holds: what is sold is the system, the layout rules and the
design, not access to a material.
