# The BSRIA report, read from the primary source — 2026-08-21

`BSRIA-Final-Report-100241-1.pdf`, published ungated on `c-ats.co.uk`. Compiled by Calum Maclean, approved by Tom
Garrigan, issued 3 March 2020. Tests carried out at BSRIA Bracknell on **24 July 2019**.

Read to settle `DOC-8` (marine provenance). It settles that, answers `DEC-1`, and raised two larger things — one of which
Neil closed the same day.

## What the report actually covers

> "Cinema Acoustic Treatment Systems Ltd commissioned BSRIA to carry out sound absorption testing on **3 materials
> in 8 different configurations** in accordance with BS EN ISO 354:2003."

| Item | As described in Table 1 |
|---|---|
| 1 | CATS Reflection Control Panel — CATS-REF-CP — **300 × 300 × 50 mm** |
| 2 | CATS Reverberation Control Panel 50 — CATS-REV-CP-50 — **300 × 300 × 50 mm** |
| 3 | CATS Resonance Control Panel — CATS-RES-CP — **600 × 600 × 50 mm** |

The eight configurations: tests 1–3 discrete items around the room (the ISO 354 standard arrangement); **tests 4 and
5 bespoke** — corners against two walls, and room edges against one wall; tests 6–8 plane absorber in the centre of
the room at 1.44 m² coverage.

## `DOC-8` — answered, and against what this repo proposed

**The marine panel was not tested.** Three materials, none of them marine; the word "marine" appears **nowhere** in
19 pages.

So `Absorption-Coefficients-CATS-REF-CP-MAR.pdf`, published on the site beside the other four, **is not from this
report**. On 2026-08-20 this repo proposed the opposite — that marine shipped "in the same April 2023 batch, so
probably the same commission". **April 2023 is the date the PDFs were published, not the date anything was tested.**

`CATS-REF-CP-MAR` is a live listed product, and marine work invokes IMO/SOLAS. **Where its absorption figures come
from is now an open question, not a documentation nicety.**

## The 50 mm in the report is a nominal — settled the same day

Table 1 records the RES-CP as **600 × 600 × 50 mm**. The number **43 does not appear anywhere in the report**. Put to
Neil as `Q82`, because if the tested article were not the shipping article that would be the range's most exposed
claim — the resonance result being, in his own words, "a very, very unusual product delivering results much deeper
panels would be proud of… probably about the most important result and discussion point in the BSRIA results pack".

His answer, 2026-08-21: **"The res-cp is and always has been 43mm deep. It's made from a 40mm foam panel bonded to a
3mm Perspex."**

So the tested article **is** the shipping article, the published coefficients stand, and BSRIA logged a rounded
nominal. The construction says where a 50 mm nominal comes from: 40 + 3.

**`DAT-1` therefore stays a data fix**, confined to `c-ats-shopify:data/panels.json` — which is what the 2026-08-20
narrowing said, and this morning's reading of it was wrong to widen it. **And the construction is new to the record:**
foam bonded to Perspex is the first statement of what the panel is made of, and it is a mechanism, so it is recorded
and not published.

## Two things the report confirms, which the record already had right

- **Mounting.** "For all tests the materials were positioned directly onto the floor of the chamber with no fixings
  between the tiles and the sample edges were left exposed." That is the free/unfixed condition the product record
  insists must travel with the figures, stated in the primary source. It corroborates `Q46`.
- **Tests 4 and 5 are client-specified, not standard.** "Bespoke and representative of how Cinema Acoustic Treatment
  Systems Ltd would install the material in real life." So citing test 4 as "the intended configuration" is correct —
  and worth saying it is our configuration, not ISO's.

## `DEC-1` — the legal name, from a third party

The report names the client throughout as **Cinema Acoustic Treatment Systems Ltd**, 272 Bath Street, Glasgow, G2
4JR. Every page header carries it. `Q26` settled that the expansion is permitted; this settles which expansion is the
legal one.
